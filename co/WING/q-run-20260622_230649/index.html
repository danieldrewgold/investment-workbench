Key debate

The market is debating whether Wingstop's domestic SSS recovery — guided to 'flat to low single-digit decline' for FY2026 after a -3% FY2025 and a worse-than-expected -8.7% Q1 2026 — is achievable in 2H 2026, and whether the unit growth engine (15-16% guided) can sustain royalty revenue growth sufficient to offset the SSS headwind and rising SG&A. The secondary debate is whether the stock's ~34x forward P/E (on consensus $4.57 FY2026 EPS) is defensible given the SSS deterioration.

Where we differ from consensus

The street's $780M FY2026 revenue consensus was set when management guided 'flat to low single-digit growth' in SSS at the Q4 2025 call (February 2026); the Q1 2026 call (April 2026) downgraded that to 'low single-digit decline' — a roughly 300-400bps adverse shift — yet consensus revenue moved only modestly (the range is $764-803M, implying the street hasn't fully reconciled the SSS downgrade against the unit-growth math). Simultaneously, the SG&A guide was raised to $146-149M including $3M of restructuring and $28M of stock comp, but the revision history (↑16/↓12 over 30 days on FY EPS) suggests the street is still anchoring near the original $140M guide. The combination of a worse SSS trajectory and higher SG&A creates a double-hit that consensus has only partially absorbed.

Narrative synthesis

Wingstop's investment thesis rests on a simple but powerful franchise royalty engine: as system-wide sales grow — driven by unit expansion and same-store sales — royalty and advertising fee revenue compounds at near-100% incremental margin above a relatively fixed SG&A base. The FY2025 actuals confirm this structure: royalty/franchise/other revenue of approximately $233M plus advertising fees of $224M totaled ~$457M of high-margin revenue against $696.9M total, with company-owned restaurant sales of ~$119M making up the remainder. The gross margin of 82.6% in the structured financials reflects this franchise-heavy mix. The analytical question for FY2026 is whether the unit growth engine can compensate for the SSS headwind — and whether consensus has correctly sized both variables.

On the SSS trajectory, the diligence trail is concerning. At the Q4 2025 earnings call (February 18, 2026), management guided domestic SSS to 'flat to low single-digit percent growth' for FY2026 — a constructive framing that implied a recovery from the -3% FY2025 result. The Q1 2026 call (April 29, 2026) then disclosed a -8.7% domestic SSS result for Q1, driven by what management attributed to 'atypical winter weather resulting in temporary restaurant closures in over 700 restaurants' and 'elevated gas prices as a result of the conflict in the Middle East.' Management updated the full-year SSS guide to 'low single-digit decline' — a roughly 300-400bps adverse revision from the February framing. The language shift is material: 'flat to low single-digit growth' implies a range of 0% to +3%; 'low single-digit decline' implies -1% to -3%. The midpoint moved from approximately +1.5% to approximately -2%, a 350bps swing. This is not a minor adjustment — on a domestic system of approximately 2,154 franchise restaurants with AUVs of ~$2.1M, a 350bps SSS swing represents roughly $158M of system-wide sales, which at a blended royalty+ad rate of approximately 9.5% (6% royalty + 3.5% ad fund, per FY2024 disclosures) translates to ~$15M of corporate revenue. That is a meaningful headwind that the consensus revenue range of $764-803M may not fully reflect.

To stress-test the consensus revenue number, consider the unit growth math. Management reiterated 15-16% global unit growth at the Q1 2026 call, consistent with the Q4 2025 guide. Starting from approximately 2,900 system-wide restaurants at end-FY2025 (2,563 at end-FY2024 plus 493 net new in FY2025 per the Q4 2025 call), 15-16% growth implies approximately 435-464 net new restaurants in FY2026, reaching roughly 3,335-3,364 by year-end. Using a mid-year average of approximately 3,130 restaurants and a blended AUV of ~$2.0M (domestic ~$2.1M, international lower), system-wide sales would be approximately $6.26B — but this assumes flat SSS. With a -2% SSS assumption (midpoint of the updated guide), system-wide sales would be approximately $6.13B. At a blended royalty+ad rate of ~9.5%, that implies corporate revenue from royalties and ad fees of approximately $582M. Adding company-owned restaurant sales (approximately 50-56 stores at ~$2.4M AUV = ~$130M) and franchise fees, total corporate revenue would be approximately $720-730M — below the consensus midpoint of $780M by $50-60M. This is the core of Claim 1: consensus appears to be modeling SSS closer to flat or mildly positive, not the -2% midpoint of the updated guide.

The SG&A line is the second angle. At the Q4 2025 call, management guided FY2026 SG&A to 'approximately $140M.' At the Q1 2026 call, this was raised to '$146-149M, which includes $3M of restructuring charges related to the corporate realignment and $28M of stock-based compensation expense.' The structured FY2025 actuals show SG&A of $128.4M (18.4% of revenue). The new guide midpoint of $147.5M represents a $19M YoY increase — a 14.8% step-up. The revision history on FY2026 EPS (↑16/↓12 over 30 days) suggests the street is actively debating this, but the net revision balance is only modestly negative, implying many analysts have not yet fully incorporated the $7-9M SG&A guide raise (from $140M to $147.5M midpoint). On a franchise model where incremental revenue flows at ~25% EBITDA margin, a $7M SG&A miss flows through at roughly 1:1 to operating income — at a 26.5% tax rate and 28.1M diluted shares, that is approximately $0.18 of EPS. This is Claim 2: the street is underweighting the SG&A guide raise.

The sequential trajectory discipline is important here. Q1 2026 revenue of $183.7M versus Q4 2025 revenue of $176M represents a +4.3% sequential increase (the table's rounded figures imply +4.6%, consistent within rounding). A same-period prior-year sequential comparison — Q1 2025 versus Q4 2024 — would be the natural benchmark, but Q4 2024 is not present in our 24-quarter historicals table (the table jumps from Q3 2024 at $162M directly to Q1 2025 at $171M), so a precise prior-year seasonal comparison is unavailable from this data set; any back-calculation from an externally sourced FY2024 full-year total would rely on data outside the verified table and should be treated as unconfirmed. What the table does confirm is that Q1 2025 itself showed a +17.4% YoY revenue gain, and Q1 2026 shows a +7.4% YoY gain — a meaningful deceleration in the year-over-year growth rate that is directionally consistent with the SSS deterioration narrative. The operating margin in Q1 2026 was 27.4% versus Q1 2025's 22.4% — but Q1 2025 included the $92.5M non-recurring gain from the UK brand partner (LPH) sale, which inflated Q1 2025 net income to $3.24 EPS. Stripping that out, Q1 2025 adjusted EPS was approximately $1.08 - $0.18 (18 cents of additional interest expense from the $500M securitization) = approximately $0.90 on a comparable basis, making Q1 2026's $1.08 reported EPS a genuine ~20% YoY improvement on an adjusted basis — consistent with the double-digit adjusted EBITDA growth management cited.

On the unit growth engine, management's credibility is high. The pipeline is at 'record levels' per the Q1 2026 call, with approximately 2,300 committed restaurants disclosed at the Q4 2025 call. The 493 net new openings in FY2025 (versus 349 in FY2024 and 255 in FY2023) demonstrates genuine acceleration. Brand partner economics remain compelling: 70%+ unlevered cash-on-cash returns on ~$580K upfront investment, with AUVs at $2.1M domestically and corporate stores approaching $2.5M. The Smart Kitchen rollout — completed across 2,500+ restaurants in 10 months — is showing a mid-single-digit SSS delta in the Southwest region (600+ restaurants) versus the national average, per the Q3 2025 and Q4 2025 calls. This is the bull case's strongest pillar: if Smart Kitchen benefits scale nationally as Club Wingstop loyalty launches, the 2H 2026 SSS recovery is plausible. The bear case is that the macro headwinds (consumer sentiment at 49.8 per U. of Michigan, personal saving rate at 2.6% — down 52.7% YoY per FRED, headline CPI at +4.2% YoY) are structural, not weather-related, and that the lower-income consumer Wingstop over-indexes to is under sustained pressure. The Q1 2026 call's attribution of the -8.7% SSS to weather and gas prices is partially credible (management estimated a ~4pp headwind from these factors), but the underlying trend ex-weather was still negative, and the macro backdrop has not materially improved.
