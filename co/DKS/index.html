Key debate

The market is debating whether the Foot Locker acquisition is a value-creating turnaround or a multi-year margin drag that permanently re-rates DKS's consolidated operating margin profile downward; simultaneously, the street must reconcile management's updated GAAP EPS guidance of $13.27-$14.27 (midpoint $13.77) against a consensus of $14.29 — a gap that implies the street is either fading GAAP acquisition costs or modeling Foot Locker profitability more optimistically than management's own guidance.

Where we differ from consensus

The street's $14.29 consensus EPS anchors to non-GAAP figures ($13.50-$14.50 non-GAAP guide) while management's updated GAAP guidance midpoint is $13.77 — a ~$0.52 gap driven by real acquisition-related costs ($97M in Q1 alone) that are recurring through the integration period, not one-time. Sell-side analysts systematically use non-GAAP as the 'clean' earnings anchor for retail names, but for DKS the GAAP-to-non-GAAP gap is unusually large and persistent given the scale of the Foot Locker integration. Additionally, the street's $22,361M FY2026 revenue consensus implies a ~$5.1B quarterly run-rate for the back half that requires Foot Locker to contribute ~$2.0-2.2B per quarter — achievable but dependent on the turnaround executing on schedule, with WSS store closures (43 closed in Q1 alone) creating a structural revenue headwind the top-down models may not fully capture.

Narrative synthesis

DICK'S Sporting Goods entered FY2026 as a fundamentally different company than it was twelve months ago — the September 2025 acquisition of Foot Locker for 9.6 million shares transformed a clean, high-margin specialty retailer into a complex, two-segment conglomerate with a turnaround embedded inside it. The analytical challenge is separating the signal from the noise: the DICK'S core business is genuinely excellent, but the Foot Locker overlay creates a margin and EPS drag that the street appears to be systematically underweighting by anchoring to non-GAAP metrics.

Start with the GAAP vs. non-GAAP reconciliation, which is the most important accounting discipline for this name. Management's Q1 2026 press release disclosed updated FY2026 GAAP EPS guidance of $13.27-$14.27 (midpoint $13.77), while simultaneously maintaining non-GAAP EPS guidance of $13.50-$14.50 (midpoint $14.00). The street consensus of $14.29 sits $0.52 above the GAAP midpoint and $0.29 above the non-GAAP midpoint — meaning consensus is not just using non-GAAP, it is above even the non-GAAP guide. In Q1 2026, the GAAP-to-non-GAAP gap was substantial: GAAP EPS of $3.54 included $174M of pre-tax litigation/settlement gains partially offset by $97M of pre-tax Foot Locker acquisition-related costs. The net effect was that GAAP EPS of $3.54 was actually ABOVE non-GAAP EPS of $2.90 in Q1 — but this was due to a one-time litigation settlement, not operational strength. Stripping that out, the underlying non-GAAP run-rate of $2.90 in Q1 implies a significant back-half weighting to reach the $13.50-$14.50 non-GAAP full-year guide. Management explicitly noted on the Q1 call that 'earnings guidance is now based on approximately 90.5 million average diluted shares outstanding' and a '27% effective tax rate for the full year' — 150bps higher than original expectations. This tax rate revision alone, applied to the full-year earnings base, represents approximately $0.15-0.20 of EPS headwind that the street may not have fully incorporated if models were built off the original 25.5% tax rate guidance from the Q4 2025 call.

The revenue picture requires careful sequential trajectory analysis. The structured financials show FY2026 (partial year, as reported through Q1 2027 in the historical table) with Q1 2027 revenue of $5,165M, Q4 2026 of $6,226M, Q3 2026 of $4,168M, and Q2 2026 of $3,647M. Note the labeling: the historical table appears to use a fiscal year convention where 'Q1 2027' is the most recent quarter (reported May 2026), which aligns with DKS's fiscal year ending in late January/early February. The street's FY2026 consensus of $22,361M implies a full fiscal year. Working from disclosed actuals: Q4 2026 ($6,226M) + Q3 2026 ($4,168M) + Q2 2026 ($3,647M) = $14,041M for three quarters. Adding Q1 2026 ($3,175M, the pre-Foot Locker quarter) gives a partial-year total. The Foot Locker acquisition closed September 8, 2025, meaning it contributed to Q3 2026 (partial quarter) and fully to Q4 2026. The Q4 2026 press release disclosed '$2.18 billion sales contribution from the newly acquired Foot Locker business' in Q4 alone. The street's $22,361M FY2026 consensus requires the remaining quarters to sum to approximately $8,320M beyond the $14,041M three-quarter total — which is broadly consistent with a full-year Foot Locker contribution of ~$8-9B annualized. However, the WSS banner closure program (43 stores closed in Q1 2027 alone, reducing from 143 to 100 stores) creates a structural revenue headwind: WSS stores averaged roughly $1.8M gross square footage for 143 stores, now reduced to 100 stores, implying a ~30% reduction in WSS revenue capacity. If WSS was generating ~$1.5-2.0B annually (estimated from store count and format), the closure program removes $450-600M of revenue — a headwind the top-down consensus models may not have granularly captured.

The Foot Locker margin trajectory is the central thesis question. Management guided Foot Locker Business operating income of $110-150M for FY2026 (raised from $100-150M at Q4 2025). On an implied Foot Locker revenue base of approximately $8-9B (annualized from Q4's $2.18B contribution), this implies a 1.2-1.9% operating margin — dramatically below the DICK'S Business's ~11% operating margin. The consolidated operating income guidance of $1.69-1.81B (GAAP, updated at Q1) versus $1.71-1.83B (non-GAAP) implies the DICK'S Business is contributing approximately $1.56-1.68B of operating income (backing out the $110-150M Foot Locker contribution). This is consistent with the DICK'S Business operating margin guidance of ~11.1% at midpoint on ~$14.5-14.7B of DICK'S revenue. The critical insight: the street's consolidated operating margin model must be implicitly assuming either (a) faster Foot Locker margin recovery than management guides, or (b) a higher DICK'S Business margin than the 11.1% midpoint. Given management raised the high-end DICK'S margin expectation to 11.4% (from 11.2%) at Q1, there is modest upside to the DICK'S segment — but not enough to offset Foot Locker's drag if the turnaround lags.

The DICK'S core business momentum is genuinely impressive and deserves credit. Q1 2027 (the most recent quarter) delivered 6.0% comp growth on top of 4.5% in the prior year — a 10.5% two-year stack. Management noted 'broad-based strength across footwear, apparel, and hardlines' with growth in both average ticket and transactions. The consistency of this comp trajectory across multiple years (5% in Q2 2025, 3.1% in Q4 2025, 6% in Q1 2026) suggests structural share gains rather than one-time factors. The World Cup tailwind (most matches on U.S. soil in June-July 2026) provides a near-term catalyst for Q2 2026 comps, which management explicitly flagged as a driver of first-half weighting. However, the Q2 2026 comp will face a tougher lap in Q2 2027 — the 5% comp from Q2 2025 was already strong, and the World Cup benefit is non-recurring. This creates a natural deceleration risk in the DICK'S Business comps in H1 2027 that the street's FY2027 revenue consensus of $23,164M (+3.6% YoY) may be underweighting.

On the macro backdrop, the setup is mixed but not alarming for DKS's core consumer. University of Michigan consumer sentiment of 44.8 (as of May 2026, down 14.2% YoY) is historically low and typically leads discretionary spending by one quarter — a genuine headwind. However, real disposable personal income is up 6.2% YoY (BEA data as of May 2026), and management explicitly noted on the Q1 2026 call that 'we continue to see a healthy consumer across income demographics with no signs of trade down.' The credit card delinquency rate of 2.92% is actually down 4.6% YoY, suggesting lower-income stress is not accelerating. The more relevant macro risk for DKS is PPI inflation of 10.1% YoY — a significant input cost pressure that could compress gross margins if vendors pass through costs and DKS cannot fully offset with pricing. Management's Q2 2025 commentary that 'we've seen some sporadic price increases, but they are surgical and not across the board' suggests they are managing this carefully, but the PPI trajectory bears watching into H2 2026.

On ownership and insider activity: the ownership structure is dominated by insiders (Ed Stack at 21.9%, William Colombo at 13.8%), creating a closely-held dynamic that limits float and active price discovery. Viking Global Investors entered as a new position of $509.4M in Q4 2025 — a meaningful conviction bet from a sophisticated long/short fund that likely reflects confidence in the Foot Locker turnaround thesis. However, insider selling is notable: Ed Stack sold $41.6M of stock in March 2026 at an average of $197.69, and CEO Lauren Hobart sold $4.6M in May 2026 at $228.12. These are not alarming in isolation (Stack retains $1.47B of stock), but the timing — shortly after the Foot Locker acquisition closed and before the full integration picture is clear — is worth monitoring. The securities litigation flagged by Kuehn Law (July 2026) alleging misrepresentation of 'a slowdown in product demand' is a tail risk that warrants tracking; if it gains traction, it could create headline noise around the Q2 2026 print. Short interest of 14% of float is elevated and creates a squeeze dynamic if Q2 2026 results beat — but also signals sophisticated money is skeptical of the Foot Locker integration thesis.

The bear case to our own view is straightforward and must be stated fairly: if the Foot Locker turnaround executes faster than management's conservative guidance implies — specifically if the Fast Break initiative (scaling to ~250 stores by back-to-school) drives meaningful comp acceleration in H2 2026 — then the $110-150M Foot Locker operating income guide could prove sandbagged, and the consolidated EPS could converge toward the non-GAAP consensus of $14.29. Management has a demonstrated history of conservative guidance: the DICK'S Business delivered non-GAAP EPS of $14.58 in FY2025, above the high end of guidance, and has beaten comp guidance consistently over the past three years. If the same sandbagging pattern applies to Foot Locker guidance, the bear case to our below-consensus EPS view is that management is being deliberately conservative on Foot Locker to set up a beat-and-raise cadence. The falsifier is Q2 2026 results (expected August 25, 2026): if Foot Locker Business operating income tracks above the $27.5-37.5M quarterly run-rate implied by the $110-150M annual guide, the turnaround is ahead of schedule and our below-consensus EPS view is wrong. The mispricing exists because sell-side analysts systematically anchor to non-GAAP metrics for retail names and have not fully internalized the GAAP-to-non-GAAP gap that the Foot Locker integration creates — this is a mechanical/modeling gap, not a fundamental disagreement about the business quality.
