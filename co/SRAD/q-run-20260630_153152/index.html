Key debate

The market is debating whether the Muddy Waters/Callisto short-seller allegations of black-market operator exposure represent a fundamental revenue risk or a non-fundamental dislocation — and separately, whether the prediction markets opportunity (Kalshi deal, CFTC developments) is a material incremental revenue driver that consensus has not yet priced.

Where we differ from consensus

The street's 3 downward EPS revisions over 30 days on FY2026 (0 up / 3 down) appear driven by the April 22 short-seller event and class action overhang rather than any fundamental model deterioration — management reaffirmed full-year guidance on April 28 and the Q1 print showed 11% revenue growth and 12% EBITDA growth. The consensus FY2026 revenue of $1,559M implies only ~20.8% growth, while management guides 23-25% constant currency (with FX-adjusted midpoint ~$1,570M), suggesting the street is not fully crediting the guide reaffirmation. More importantly, the Kalshi partnership (June 8, 2026) — which positions Sportradar as the core data/infrastructure provider for prediction markets — is a post-Q1 development that no sell-side model has had time to incorporate, and management explicitly flagged prediction market revenue as a 'biggest' confidence driver for back-half 2026 guidance.

Narrative synthesis

Sportradar's business model is structurally more defensible than the current share price implies, and the analytical work required to see this involves reconciling three separate layers: the GAAP vs. non-GAAP margin gap, the sequential trajectory discipline on Q1 results, and the post-Q1 Kalshi catalyst that consensus has not yet absorbed.

Start with the GAAP vs. non-GAAP reconciliation, which is the most important accounting discipline for this name. The structured financials show FY2025 GAAP gross margin of 21.1% ($261.5M on $1,239.1M revenue) and GAAP operating margin of 9.1% ($112.3M). Management, however, reports Adjusted EBITDA of €297M on €1,290M revenue — an Adjusted EBITDA margin of 23.0%. The gap between GAAP operating income ($112.3M) and Adjusted EBITDA (~$297M equivalent) is approximately $185M, which reflects D&A of $64.3M plus stock-based compensation and other non-cash/non-recurring items. The GAAP gross margin of 21.1% vs. the deck's implied non-GAAP gross margin of ~52-57% (per the quarterly historical table showing Q1 2026 GM% of 52.8%) is a massive 30+ percentage point gap — this is structural to Sportradar's economics, where sports rights costs (~35% of revenue per deck p.17) are classified below gross profit in GAAP but excluded from Adjusted EBITDA. Analysts modeling this name on GAAP gross margin alone would dramatically misread the unit economics. The correct lens is Adjusted EBITDA margin, which has expanded from 17% (FY2022) to 23% (FY2025) — a 600bps improvement over three years — and management guides 25.0-25.3% for FY2026.

On sequential trajectory, the Q1 2026 print requires careful handling. Revenue of $396M in Q1 2026 was down 6.1% sequentially from Q4 2025's $421M. A naive read calls this deceleration. But the prior-year sequential comparison is essential: Q1 2025 was $356M vs. Q4 2024 (not directly in the table, but Q1 2025 was the base quarter). The Q4 2025 to Q1 2026 sequential decline of -6.1% must be compared against the Q4 2024 to Q1 2025 sequential. From the quarterly table, Q1 2025 was $356M and Q2 2025 was $363M (+2.1%), Q3 2025 was $334M (-8.1%), Q4 2025 was $421M (+26.3%). The Q4 2025 spike (+26.3%) was driven by the IMG Arena acquisition closing November 1, 2025 — so the Q1 2026 sequential decline from Q4 2025 is partly a normalization from the IMG step-up, not organic deceleration. Management explicitly flagged on the Q4 2025 call that 'given the timing of sporting events, including IMG content, we anticipate the strongest revenue growth in Q2 and Q3 of this year' — meaning Q1 is seasonally the weakest quarter for IMG content. The YoY growth of +11.3% in Q1 2026 is the cleaner signal, and it is consistent with the full-year guide of 23-25% given management's explicit back-half weighting.

The Muddy Waters/Callisto short-seller event of April 22, 2026 — which caused a 22% single-day stock collapse — is the central overhang and requires serious engagement rather than dismissal. The allegations, as described across the class action filings, center on: (1) Sportradar intentionally working with black-market gambling operators, (2) misrepresentation of compliance practices, and (3) revenue derived from unlicensed/illegal operators. Evaluating these claims: (1) The mechanism alleged is that Sportradar knowingly supplied data to operators in jurisdictions where those operators lack licenses. Management's response on the Q1 2026 call was categorical denial — 'Sportradar and I reject the unfounded and misinformed allegations' — but notably, management did not provide specific quantification of revenue from licensed vs. unlicensed operators when directly asked by an analyst ('Curious if you're willing to quantify and say with confidence what your revenue mix is for operators in black illegal markets'). The CFO's non-answer on this specific question is a yellow flag. (2) The falsifier for the bear case is regulatory action: if Sportradar's licenses in key jurisdictions (they hold regulatory licenses in 'jurisdictions around the world' per the Q1 call) are challenged or revoked, the revenue impact would be material. No such action has been disclosed. (3) The probability-weighted impact: if 10-15% of revenue were from operators later deemed non-compliant and subject to contract termination, that would be $124-186M of revenue at risk — a material but not existential number. The class action deadline of July 17, 2026 is the near-term catalyst; if no regulatory action accompanies the legal filings, the overhang likely fades. The insider buying pattern is the strongest counter-signal: CEO Karsten Koerl bought $10.02M of stock at an average of $13.33 across April 30 - May 6, 2026 — immediately after the short-seller event — and five other directors bought simultaneously. This is coordinated, open-market purchasing at post-collapse prices, not RSU vesting. A CEO buying $10M of stock personally after a short-seller attack is a high-conviction signal that the allegations are not internally viewed as existential.

The Kalshi prediction markets partnership (announced June 8, 2026, per StockTwits and news flow) is the most important post-Q1 development and the one most likely to be unmodeled by consensus. Management on the Q1 2026 call explicitly cited prediction market revenue as 'probably the biggest' confidence driver for hitting full-year guidance, noting 'we have really good sight lines right now we think with regards to some prediction market revenue opportunity that's going to happen in the predominantly back half of the year.' The Kalshi deal — positioning Sportradar as the core data and solutions provider across premier sports leagues for prediction markets — was announced after the Q1 earnings call and after the consensus snapshot. The three FY2026 EPS revisions (all downward, 0 upward) over the 30 days ending June 30 predate any model update for Kalshi. The TAM for prediction markets is unquantified by management, but the structural logic is compelling: Sportradar already has the official data rights, the odds infrastructure, and the integrity services that prediction market exchanges require — Kalshi is a distribution channel, not a new product build. The incremental revenue from this partnership in H2 2026 is genuinely unmodeled by the street.

On the FY2027 consensus EPS of $0.75 (YoY +69.7% from $0.44), the street's implied margin trajectory deserves scrutiny. Management guides a long-term Adjusted EBITDA margin target of 30%+ beyond 2026, from 25.0-25.3% in FY2026. If FY2027 revenue grows at the consensus 14.2% to ~$1,780M, and Adjusted EBITDA margin expands 200-300bps to ~27-28%, Adjusted EBITDA would be approximately $481-498M. Translating to GAAP EPS requires the GAAP bridge: D&A of ~$70M (growing modestly), stock comp of ~$80-90M (estimated from the GAAP-to-non-GAAP gap), net interest expense of ~$50-60M (declining as debt is zero and cash builds), and a 15-16% tax rate. This math produces GAAP net income of approximately $200-220M on ~290M diluted shares (after $250M buyback program reduces count), implying EPS of $0.69-0.76 — roughly in line with consensus $0.75. However, if the prediction markets revenue ramp is material (even $50-100M incremental in FY2027 at high incremental margins), the EPS could be $0.85-0.95, well above the $0.87 top of the consensus range. The street's FY2027 estimate appears to model the base business correctly but assigns zero value to prediction markets optionality.

The ownership structure is notable: Canada Pension Plan Investment Board holds 31.4% and CEO Koerl holds 31.6% — together over 63% of shares are in two hands. True float is thin (~68% public float per the ownership table, but with CPPIB and Koerl holding 63%, the tradeable float is closer to 35-40% of shares outstanding). The mispricing, in our read, is mechanical/flow-driven: the 22% single-day collapse on April 22 was a short-seller-triggered sentiment event, the subsequent class action filing cascade is legal boilerplate (not evidence of regulatory action), and the insider buying cluster on April 30 - May 6 at $12.49-$13.33 is the strongest available signal that the company's own leadership views the allegations as non-existential.
