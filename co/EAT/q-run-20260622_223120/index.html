Thesis & brief

research/deep_research.py ↗

Key debate

The market is debating whether Chili's mid-single-digit SSS trajectory is sustainable as it laps increasingly difficult two-year stacks (Q3 FY25 was +31.6%), and whether restaurant-level margins can expand 30-40bps annually as management guides despite commodity inflation running at mid-single digits and structural cost pressures in R&M, delivery, and rent. The secondary debate is whether consensus FY2027 EPS of $12.48 (+15.9% YoY) is achievable given the comp difficulty and margin headwinds becoming more visible.

Where we differ from consensus

Sell-side models FY2027 EPS top-down from a ~16% growth rate anchored to FY2026 momentum, but the Q3 FY2026 cost bridge (deck pp.5-7) shows three structural cost lines — R&M, delivery/to-go, and rent — each inflating 20-30bps YoY independent of sales volume, totaling +80bps of structural restaurant expense pressure that leverage only partially offsets (-50bps). With PPI running +13.1% YoY (FRED, as of May 2026) and Brinker's own Q3 FY2026 food cost bridge showing +130bps commodity headwind offset by only -120bps menu price, the net food cost drag is likely to widen in FY2027 as the company's pricing power moderates (food-away-from-home CPI only +3.5% YoY per BLS). The street's FY2027 margin expansion assumption of ~30-40bps appears to ignore these compounding structural pressures, and the buyback-driven share count reduction that powered FY2026 EPS growth is already largely reflected in consensus share count assumptions.

Narrative synthesis

Brinker International's Chili's brand has executed one of the most impressive casual dining turnarounds in recent memory — from a $0.84 adjusted EPS quarter in Q1 FY2025 to a $2.87 adjusted EPS in Q3 FY2026, a ~242% improvement in two years driven by the combination of 20 consecutive quarters of SSS growth, AUV approaching $5M per unit, and restaurant-level margin expansion from ~11% to ~18%. The question for institutional investors today is not whether the turnaround happened — it clearly did — but whether the consensus FY2027 EPS estimate of $12.48 (+15.9% YoY from FY2026's $10.76) is achievable given the structural cost pressures now visible in the granular cost bridge data, the comp difficulty of lapping two-year stacks in the 35-40% range, and the moderating pricing environment.

Beginning with the SSS trajectory, the sequential deceleration from Q1 FY2026 (+21.4%) to Q2 FY2026 (+8.6%) to Q3 FY2026 (+4.0%) is the most visible data point in the corpus. The critical discipline here is to compare this sequential pattern against the prior-year same-period sequential to determine whether this is seasonal-normal or a real break. Q1 FY2025 Chili's SSS was +14.1%, Q2 FY2025 was +31.4%, Q3 FY2025 was +31.6% — so the FY2026 deceleration is entirely a function of lapping the extraordinary FY2025 acceleration, not a deterioration in underlying demand. The two-year cumulative comp through Q3 FY2026 is approximately 35.6% (4.0% + 31.6%), which is actually stronger than the Q1 FY2026 two-year stack of 35.5% (21.4% + 14.1%). On this basis, the underlying business is holding its gains. Management's April 2026 commentary that 'outperformance versus the industry is accelerating, from 320 basis points better in February to 550 points in March, and now 560 points month to date through April' provides real-time confirmation that the comp trajectory is not deteriorating on an industry-relative basis. For Q4 FY2026, management guided mid-single-digit SSS, and the current quarter consensus of $1,536M revenue (+5.1% YoY) is consistent with that guide. The Q4 FY2025 Chili's SSS was +23.7%, so Q4 FY2026 lapping that creates a two-year stack of approximately 28-29% if mid-single-digit materializes — still robust but the easiest comp of the year is now behind them.

The more interesting analytical question — and the one where we believe consensus has a blind spot — is the FY2027 margin trajectory. Management guided on the Q3 FY2026 call to 30-40bps of restaurant operating margin expansion for FY2026 as a whole, and CFO Micah Ware expressed confidence in that target. But the Q3 FY2026 cost bridge data (deck pp.5-7) reveals a structural problem that will compound into FY2027. The restaurant expense bridge shows: R&M +30bps, delivery/to-go +30bps, rent +20bps, other +20bps, partially offset by leverage -50bps, for a net +50bps headwind. Critically, three of the four cost pressures (R&M, delivery/to-go, rent) are structural and NOT leverage-sensitive — they will not improve proportionally with higher sales. R&M is a function of restaurant age and maintenance cycle; delivery/to-go reflects the structural shift in off-premise mix; rent is contractual. Even if Chili's SSS runs at +5% in FY2027, the leverage benefit on these fixed costs is limited. The food cost bridge is equally concerning: commodity costs were +130bps headwind in Q3 FY2026, offset by only -120bps of menu price, for a net +60bps food cost inflation. With PPI running +13.1% YoY as of May 2026 (FRED data), and management's own Q3 FY2026 guidance update noting commodity inflation 'in the mid-single digits' (up from 'low single digits' in Q2), the food cost pressure is accelerating, not moderating. The BLS data shows leisure and hospitality average hourly earnings up +4.0% YoY as of May 2026, while food-away-from-home CPI is only +3.5% — meaning labor cost inflation is outpacing the industry's pricing power, creating a structural margin squeeze.

To quantify the FY2027 margin impact: if restaurant expense structural costs (R&M, delivery/to-go, rent) continue to inflate at +80bps gross with only -50bps leverage offset (assuming ~5% SSS), the net restaurant expense headwind is approximately +30bps. If food costs remain elevated with commodity PPI at double-digit levels and menu price contribution moderating to ~3-4% (as the company maintains its $3-4 below-competition value positioning), food cost could add another +30-50bps headwind. Against management's stated 30-40bps expansion target for FY2026, the FY2027 path to similar expansion requires either (a) significant commodity deflation, (b) accelerating SSS above mid-single-digits to generate more leverage, or (c) labor cost improvement. None of these is clearly in the consensus model. Our estimate is that FY2027 restaurant operating margin expansion is more likely to be flat to +10bps rather than the +30-40bps the street appears to embed in its $12.48 EPS estimate.

On the revenue side, the FY2026 management guidance of $5.78-5.82B (midpoint $5.80B) compares to consensus of $5.808B — the street is essentially at the guide midpoint, which is appropriate given the Q3 FY2026 actual of $1.470B and the Q4 FY2026 consensus of $1,536M. Summing the four quarters: Q1 $1,349M + Q2 $1,452M + Q3 $1,470M + Q4 $1,536M = $5,807M, which is exactly at consensus and within management's guidance range. For FY2027, consensus assumes $6,145M (+5.8% YoY). This requires approximately $337M of incremental revenue, which at ~1,200 Chili's company-owned units and ~$5M AUV implies roughly 5-6% SSS plus modest unit growth (management guided 6 company-owned openings for FY2026, with international franchise growth of 24-27 units). The revenue consensus appears reasonable on its face, but the EPS bridge from $10.76 to $12.48 requires $1.72 of incremental EPS — approximately 16% growth. With ~44.7-45M diluted shares in FY2026 and continued buybacks, the share count could decline to ~42-43M in FY2027, contributing roughly $0.25-0.35 of EPS mechanically. The remaining $1.37-1.47 of EPS growth must come from operating income improvement, which requires approximately $60-65M of incremental operating income on a base of ~$512M (FY2025 actual). That is achievable if margins expand, but our analysis suggests the structural cost pressures make 30-40bps of margin expansion unlikely — more realistic is flat to +10bps, which would generate only $20-30M of incremental operating income from margin, with the remainder needing to come from revenue growth alone.

The insider activity pattern warrants explicit attention. In January-February 2026, CEO Kevin Hochman sold $10.58M of stock (25.2% of his pre-sale stake) at an average of $160.31, CMO George Felix sold 56.4% of his stake at $161.00, CFO Micah Ware sold 20.1% of her stake at $162.40, and COO Aaron White sold 14.1% of his stake at $165.00. This was a coordinated, broad-based insider sale across the entire C-suite at prices in the $159-167 range — approximately 10-15% above the current stock price of ~$143-146. Director Frances Allen has continued selling through May-June 2026 at $135-144. The GuruFocus data notes '23 insider sells and no buys at Brinker International over the past year.' This is not a single executive diversifying — it is the entire management team selling meaningful percentages of their stakes at prices well above current levels. The bear-read is that management, who have the best visibility into the FY2027 cost and comp trajectory, were net sellers at $160+ and the stock has since declined to $143-146. The bull-read is that these were planned sales after a massive stock appreciation (EAT went from ~$30 in 2023 to $160+ in early 2026). We note the pattern but do not treat it as dispositive — the more important signal is the cost bridge data, which is observable and quantifiable.

On the macro backdrop, the setup for casual dining is mixed. Real disposable personal income is up +5.8% YoY (BEA, April 2026), which is a genuine tailwind for restaurant traffic. However, consumer sentiment (University of Michigan) has collapsed to 49.8 as of April 2026, down from the prior year — historically a leading indicator for discretionary spending with approximately a one-quarter lag. The personal saving rate has fallen to 2.60% (April 2026), down 52.7% YoY, suggesting consumers are already drawing down buffers. Credit card delinquency rates at 2.92% are slightly below prior year, but the combination of low savings, weak sentiment, and headline CPI at +4.2% YoY creates a consumer environment where Chili's value positioning ($3-4 below casual dining competition) is actually a relative advantage — the company is likely to continue gaining share from higher-priced casual dining peers even if absolute traffic softens. This is the bull case that the street correctly identifies. The risk is that if consumer stress intensifies, even value-positioned casual dining sees traffic declines, as occurred in 2008-2009.

The institutional ownership structure is unremarkable — BlackRock (15.3%), Fidelity (15.2%), and Vanguard (11.2%) dominate, with no strategic or activist holder. The crowding score of 0/100 with only 15/47 tracked hedge funds holding the name suggests the stock is not over-owned by fast money, which is a positive setup for the thesis. Millennium Management's +$145.8M addition in Q4 2025 (a +3,725% increase) is the most notable smart-money signal, though 13F data is 45 days stale. The short interest at 19% of float is elevated and creates potential for a squeeze if Q4 FY2026 prints above consensus — but it also reflects meaningful institutional skepticism about the sustainability of the comp trajectory. The early note redemption of the 8.25% Senior Notes due 2030 (noted in the June 18, 2026 news) is a positive capital structure signal — management is using strong free cash flow to reduce high-cost debt, which will reduce net interest expense and provide a modest EPS tailwind in FY2027.

Synthesizing the evidence: we are modestly below consensus on FY2027 EPS ($11.80-12.00 vs. consensus $12.48) driven by our view that restaurant operating margin expansion will be +0-10bps rather than the +30-40bps the street appears to embed, due to structural non-leverage-sensitive cost inflation in R&M, delivery/to-go, and rent, compounded by commodity PPI running at +13.1% YoY.
