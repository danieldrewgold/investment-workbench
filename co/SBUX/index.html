Thesis & brief

research/deep_research.py ↗

Key debate

The market is debating whether the Q2 FY2026 comp acceleration (+7.1% US, +6.2% global) is durable and whether the China deconsolidation creates a structural margin inflection that consensus is underpricing — or whether the comp recovery is front-loaded on easy laps and macro headwinds (University of Michigan sentiment at 49.8, personal saving rate collapsed to 2.6%) will cap the recovery trajectory. The secondary debate is whether the $2.0B restructuring charge disclosed in the May 2026 8-K signals a cost-takeout that flows to FY2027 EPS beyond what the street models.

Where we differ from consensus

Sell-side models are anchored to the pre-deconsolidation revenue base and are modeling China as a revenue headwind without fully crediting the margin-mix improvement: removing ~$2-3B of company-operated China revenue (which ran at sub-5% operating margins in a competitive market) and replacing it with a royalty/licensing stream is structurally accretive to consolidated operating margins by 100-200bps, but consensus operating margin for FY2027 appears to embed only modest recovery. Additionally, the May 2026 8-K disclosed a $2.0B restructuring charge tied to streamlining domestic and international support organizations as the business moves toward 90% licensed — the run-rate savings from this action are not yet visible in consensus opex models, which are anchored to the FY2025 SG&A base of $2,617M. The 15 upward EPS revisions vs. 1 downward over 30 days confirms the street is directionally moving toward our view but has not yet fully repriced the structural margin shift.

Narrative synthesis

Starbucks enters the second half of FY2026 with genuine operational momentum for the first time in over two years, but the more analytically interesting question is not whether the turnaround is real — it clearly is — but whether the structural P&L transformation from the China JV deconsolidation and the May 2026 restructuring creates a margin inflection that consensus FY2027 models have not fully absorbed. Let me walk through the diligence trail.

Starting with the operational recovery: the sequential trajectory discipline requires comparing Q2 FY2026 vs. Q1 FY2026 against the prior-year equivalent. Q2 FY2026 revenue of $9,532M vs. Q1 FY2026 of $9,915M = -3.9% sequential. Prior year: Q2 FY2025 of $8,762M vs. Q1 FY2025 of $9,398M = -6.8% sequential. The current-year Q2 sequential decline of -3.9% is actually 290bps BETTER than the prior-year seasonal pattern of -6.8%, meaning the business is outperforming its own seasonal baseline — this is not deceleration, it is acceleration relative to pattern. The Q2 FY2026 operating margin of 8.7% vs. Q1's 9.0% follows the same seasonal logic: Q2 FY2025 was 6.9% vs. Q1 FY2025's 11.9%, so the current-year Q2 margin of 8.7% represents a massive improvement over the prior-year Q2 of 6.9% (+180bps YoY) even as it declined sequentially from Q1. The YoY operating income growth of +37.8% in Q2 FY2026 confirms the turnaround is real and accelerating.

The China deconsolidation is the structural event the street is still calibrating. The Boyu Capital JV closed April 2, 2026 (per the press release), meaning Q3 FY2026 (beginning April) is the first quarter without China company-operated revenue consolidated. In Q2 FY2026, CFO Kathy Smith disclosed: 'Beginning in Q3, the retail operations of Starbucks China will be deconsolidated from our financials and will be reported as part of our broader licensed portfolio. We will also cease our quarterly reporting on China standalone.' China had approximately 8,000 company-operated stores at the time of closing. Using the Q1 FY2026 international segment revenue of $2.1B growing 10% YoY, and noting that China was the largest single international market, a reasonable estimate (labeled as inferred, not cited) is that China contributed $700-900M per quarter in company-operated revenue, or roughly $2.8-3.6B annualized. This revenue disappears from the consolidated P&L in H2 FY2026 and fully in FY2027, replaced by a royalty/licensing stream. Starbucks retains 40% equity in the JV and licenses the brand/IP. The royalty rate is not explicitly disclosed, but the structural economics are clear: company-operated China margins were sub-5% (management noted China was 'improving profitability' in Q4 FY2025 but it remained a drag), while a licensing royalty on system sales flows at near-100% margin to the licensor. The net EPS effect of this swap is positive even as reported revenue declines — a dynamic that top-down revenue-focused consensus models systematically underweight.

The May 2026 restructuring is the second underappreciated driver. The 8-K filed May 15, 2026 disclosed a $2.0B restructuring charge tied to 'further streamlining its domestic and international support organization' as 'the business has moved towards a model where nearly 90% of its coffeehouses are licensed.' This follows a September 2025 8-K for a $1.0B charge. The June 2026 news reports of corporate job cuts in London (~60 positions in Hong Kong's Asia-Pacific office) and London offices confirm the restructuring is actively executing. The critical analytical question is: what is the run-rate annual savings from these actions? The $2.0B charge is a one-time cost, not a run-rate savings figure. However, the mechanism — eliminating international support infrastructure as licensees take over operations — is structurally permanent. FY2025 SG&A was $2,617M (7.0% of revenue). If the restructuring removes 5-8% of the support cost base (a conservative estimate given the scale of the charge and the explicit shift to 90% licensed), that implies $130-210M of annual SG&A savings. At a 25.9% tax rate and ~1,140M diluted shares, $130-210M of pre-tax savings = $0.08-$0.14 of EPS accretion. Consensus FY2027 EPS of $3.01 does not appear to fully embed this, given that the restructuring charge was only disclosed in May 2026 and the 15 upward revisions vs. 1 downward over 30 days suggests analysts are still updating models.

On the revenue side, the consensus FY2026 revenue estimate of $37,615M (+1.2% YoY from FY2025's $37,184M) and FY2027 of $38,326M (+1.9%) appear to already reflect the China deconsolidation headwind — the modest growth rates are consistent with losing $2-3B of China company-operated revenue while growing the remaining base. Management raised FY2026 comp guidance to '5% or better' globally and '5% or better' in the US after Q2, up from the initial $2.15-$2.40 EPS guide to $2.25-$2.45. The Q2 US comp of +7.1% (transactions +4%, ticket +3%) is running well above the 5% guidance floor, suggesting the guide is conservative. The beat history supports this: Q1 FY2026 delivered 4% global comps vs. what was a cautious setup, and Q2 delivered 6.2% global comps. Management's pattern under Niccol has been to guide conservatively and beat — the Q2 call explicitly noted 'positive comp trends have continued through April' as the basis for raising guidance.

The macro context creates a genuine tension in the thesis. University of Michigan consumer sentiment at 49.8 (down 4.6% YoY as of April 2026) is at recessionary levels. The personal saving rate has collapsed to 2.6% (down 52.7% YoY), meaning consumers are drawing down buffers. Food away from home CPI is running +3.5% YoY, which provides a pricing tailwind but also a consumer affordability headwind. Leisure & Hospitality average hourly earnings are up +4.0% YoY vs. food away from home CPI of +3.5% — labor costs are outpacing menu price inflation by ~50bps, a margin compression dynamic. However, Starbucks' Q2 performance (+7.1% US comps in this macro environment) suggests the brand is recapturing lapsed customers through the Green Apron service improvement rather than relying on macro tailwinds. The 35.5M Starbucks Rewards 90-day active members (a record, per Q1 FY2026 call) and cold foam platform sales up +40% in Q2 suggest genuine demand recovery rather than macro-driven spending. The bear case — that macro deterioration caps the recovery — is a real risk but is being actively disproved in real-time by the comp data.

On the GAAP vs. non-GAAP reconciliation: the structured financials show FY2025 operating margin of 7.9% (GAAP) and net margin of 5.0%. Management's Q2 FY2026 call cited non-GAAP operating margin of 9.4% for Q2, while the historical table shows GAAP operating margin of 8.7% for Q2 FY2026. The ~70bps gap reflects restructuring charges and transaction costs excluded from non-GAAP. The $2.0B restructuring charge (May 2026 8-K) will flow through GAAP results in H2 FY2026 and potentially into FY2027, creating a significant GAAP vs. non-GAAP divergence. Consensus EPS of $2.38 for FY2026 and $3.01 for FY2027 appear to be non-GAAP estimates (consistent with sell-side practice for turnaround names with large restructuring charges). Investors should be aware that GAAP EPS will be materially lower in FY2026 due to the $2.0B charge.

On ownership and institutional positioning: Capital Group holds 19.4% of shares — an unusually large active manager concentration that signals high-conviction institutional ownership. The 13F data shows Citadel increased its position by 120% and Renaissance Technologies entered as a new position in Q4 2025, while Lone Pine Capital fully exited (-$470.5M). The Lone Pine exit is notable — Lone Pine is a high-quality long/short fund that had been a holder for multiple quarters, and a full exit at the Q4 2025 trough (when GAAP EPS was $0.12 in Q4 FY2025) may reflect frustration with the pace of recovery rather than a structural bear thesis. The subsequent Q1 and Q2 FY2026 results (EPS of $0.26 and $0.45 respectively, with comps accelerating) suggest Lone Pine may have exited at the wrong time — both quarters delivered meaningful EPS recovery off the Q4 FY2025 trough, and Q2 FY2026 operating income grew +37.8% YoY. Insider selling has been modest: Brady Brewer (CEO International) sold $0.87M across 7 filings at an average of $97.79, and Sara Kelly (EVP) sold $0.45M — these are small relative to retained stakes and appear to be diversification rather than conviction sales.

Synthesizing: the FY2026 consensus EPS of $2.38 is essentially in line with management's raised midpoint of $2.35 ($2.25-$2.45 range), so there is no meaningful FY2026 edge. The edge is in FY2027, where consensus at $3.01 (+26.4% YoY) embeds a recovery that is achievable but may be underestimating the structural margin benefit from (1) China deconsolidation mix improvement, (2) restructuring-driven SG&A savings of $130-210M annualized, and (3) operating leverage on a recovering US comp base. Our FY2027 EPS estimate of $3.20-$3.35 represents 6-11% upside to consensus, driven primarily by margin rather than revenue. The key risk is that macro deterioration (sentiment at 49.8, saving rate at 2.6%) causes the US comp recovery to stall in H2 FY2026, which would compress the FY2027 earnings base and make the consensus $3.01 look optimistic rather than conservative.
