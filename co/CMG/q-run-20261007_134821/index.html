Thesis & brief

research/deep_research.py ↗

Key debate

Is the 2026 'Recipe for Growth' comp recovery (+0.5% Q1, +2.2% Q2, guide raised to low-single-digit) durable enough to support a 2027 EPS rebound of +19%? Or is CMG spending heavily on marketing, labor and equipment, and pricing below inflation, to buy 1-2% comps, while food-safety headlines (cyclospora, salmonella suit) cap the recovery?

Where we differ from consensus

Sell-side 2027 numbers have moved up only (5 up / 0 down over 30 days), extrapolating the Q2 beat-and-raise. They model 2027 as a 'normalization' year where 2026 investments lap and margin recovers. Management, however, said on the record that HEEP time savings are being reinvested in restaurants and that pricing is deliberately below inflation. Meanwhile leisure-and-hospitality wages (+3.9%) exceed food-away-from-home CPI (+3.4%), which exceeds CMG's ~2-2.5% price. Separately, the Q3 consensus of $3,280M (+9.2%) does not reconcile to the CFO's statement that Q3 expense guidance was built on a '1% guide' that assumed the cyclospora impact continued through the quarter.

Narrative synthesis

The setup. Chipotle trades in the low-$30s. That level is inferred from the $35.75 August insider sale and October retail commentary about adding 'in the 30s'; it is not a verified quote. The stock sits near 52-week lows, while the sell-side holds a $43.76 mean target, $1.15 FY2026 EPS and $1.37 FY2027 EPS (+19%). My view splits the business into two halves:
- **Revenue engine: fine and arguably improving.** The 2026 transaction recovery, the HEEP rollout and ~8-9% unit growth are real.
- **Earnings conversion: still impaired, and the Street has not internalized it.** Consensus 2027 assumes a margin rebuild that management's own stated strategy argues against.
Near term, Q3 consensus revenue sits above what the CFO's cyclospora-embedded guide implies. My estimates are $3,245M Q3 revenue, $1.13 FY26 EPS and $1.29 FY27 EPS. That makes this an estimate-cut call rather than a short: at ~$31 the stock already trades at ~24x my 2027 number. CMG has rarely offered that multiple over the past decade, though that comparison is analyst knowledge, not in the corpus.

Reconciliation first, because the base matters. The structured financials show FY2025 diluted EPS of $1.09. The same block's net income of $1,535.8M over 1,265.4M diluted shares gives $1.21, which is internally inconsistent; the 1,265.4M figure is likely a period-end count rather than the weighted average. The quarterly GAAP EPS (0.28 + 0.32 + 0.29 + 0.25) sums to $1.14, and management's adjusted FY2025 EPS was $1.17. Consensus's -1.7% FY2026 growth is computed off the $1.17 adjusted base, so I benchmark everything on an adjusted basis.

On margins, the structured 25.4% 'gross margin' is essentially restaurant-level margin; the four 2025 quarters averaged ~25.4% (26.2, 27.4, 24.5, 23.4). The 16.9% operating margin reconciles exactly: 25.4% less 5.5% SG&A less 3.0% D&A. Two items now sit outside restaurant-level margin and are growing:
- **Pre-opening plus impairment** ran 0.9% of revenue in Q2 2026, versus 0.5% in Q2 2025 (deck p.5).
- **Adjusted G&A** grew from $159.9M to $176.2M (+10%), and Q1 GAAP G&A was 6.6% of revenue versus 6.0%.

Q2's flat $0.33 adjusted EPS hides a 7% decline in adjusted net income ($418.9M vs $450.4M). The EPS line was held flat by a lower share count, not by operations.

The Q3 revenue diligence. Q2 2026 revenue grew +8.4% sequentially versus +6.5% in the same period of 2025, so that acceleration was real; I am not arguing the recovery is fake. Next, decompose non-comp growth (revenue growth minus comp):
- Q1 2026: 7.4% - 0.5% = 6.9pp
- Q2 2026: 9.3% - 2.2% = 7.1pp
- Q3 2025: 7.5% - 0.3% = 7.2pp

Units are growing ~8-9% at ~80% productivity, so ~7pp from units is stable.

On the Q2 call, the CFO confirmed that Q3 expense guidance 'was based off of, was that 1% guide because... it assumes the continuation of that impact through the rest of the quarter.' A 1% comp on the $3,003M Q3 2025 base, plus ~7.1pp from units, gives ~$3,245M. Consensus of $3,280M (+9.2%) requires ~2.1% comp, a continuation of Q2's rate as if cyclospora never happened.

Four-way sequential check:
- **Consensus:** $3,280M vs Q2's $3,349M is -2.1% sequential, matching Q3 2025's -2.0% (Q3 2024 was -6.0%). Consensus is modeling a seasonally normal quarter.
- **My number:** -3.1% sequential, ~110bps worse than seasonal. That gap maps directly onto a ~120bp comp step-down from 2.2% to ~1%.

The mix is worse than the headline. Q3 pricing is guided to 'mid-2%' and mix has been a drag of 40-100bps (Q1 check -0.1% on ~0.9% price; Q2 check +1.2% on 1.6% price). A 1% comp therefore implies transactions of roughly -1%, breaking the positive-transaction streak the CEO has led every 2026 call with.

The news flow argues the impact did not fade. A salmonella lawsuit surfaced on August 11, and on October 5 'food-safety headlines return ahead of late-October results' coincided with a 4-6% drop and unusual put buying. Yet Q3 consensus revisions over 30 days were 3 up / 1 down, so the Street has not stepped toward this view.

The Q3 and FY2026 EPS bridge. Q3 2025 operating margin was 15.9%. Q2 2026 operating margin fell 250bps year over year (RLM -220bps), and Q1 fell 380bps (RLM -250bps adjusted).
- **Consensus Q3 EPS of $0.29:** at ~1,255M diluted shares and a 24-26% tax rate, this implies ~$467M operating income, or ~14.2% margin, only -170bps year over year.
- **My Q3:**
  - Pricing improves to mid-2% and H2 cost inflation steps down to 'low-to-mid-single-digit', which is real help.
  - But a ~1% comp gives less fixed-cost leverage on labor and occupancy than Q2's 2.2%, and G&A is running +10% or more.
  - Net: ~-210bps, or ~13.8% on $3,245M, which is ~$448M operating income and ~$0.28 EPS.
- **Q4:** consensus $0.28 looks reasonable. The 2025 base included a $27M gift-card true-up worth 70bps of RLM; ex-breakage, the margin compare is roughly flat, and the -2.5% comp compare is easy.
- **FY2026:** 0.24 + 0.33 + 0.28 + 0.28 gives ~$1.13 versus $1.15. This is a small gap, but its direction tells you what the Street is under-modeling: deleverage.

The real disagreement is FY2027. Back-solving consensus: $1.37 × ~1,225M shares is ~$1,678M net income, or ~$2,238M pretax at 25% tax, or ~$2,198M operating income on $14,443M revenue. That is a ~15.2% operating margin, versus ~14.2% implied for 2026, so roughly +100bps of expansion.

The revenue line is defensible:
- Consensus +10.9% needs ~3.5% comp on top of ~7pp from units.
- HEEP is the best support. Management cites 'hundreds of basis points of improvement in comp sales' at equipped stores, with ~2 months to ramp. About 1,000 of ~4,200 restaurants were equipped by Q2 (~24%), heading to 2,000 by year-end and completion 'some time in 2027'.
- My inference, since management has declined to quantify the lift: a ~200-300bp, one-year lift per store implies roughly 60-100bps of incremental system comp in 2027. That gets me to ~3% comp and $14.3B revenue, most of the gap to consensus being the lower Q3 base.

The margin line is where consensus breaks, for four reasons:
1. **HEEP savings do not drop to margin.** CEO Boatwright, Q2 2026: 'Because we're reinvesting the labor efficiency back into our restaurants, our crews can spend more time with our guests.' On Q1: 'pricing below inflation because we believe reinforcing our value.'
2. **The price-versus-cost arithmetic is negative.** Leisure-and-hospitality wages are +3.9% (BLS) and PPI is +9.9% year over year, with beef and freight already cited in Q2. A ~2.5% price against ~3-3.5% blended cost inflation on a ~76% cost base compresses RLM ~50bps before leverage.
3. **Traffic leverage roughly offsets it, and no more.** +1% traffic at ~40% flow-through adds back ~40bps, and G&A leverage adds perhaps 20bps. Net: ~+35bps of operating margin, to ~14.35%, which is $1.28-1.29 of EPS, not $1.37.
4. **Peers flag the outlier.** TXRH converts +11.2% revenue growth into +7.6% EPS growth, DRI +3.7% into +5.8%, and CAVA +27.5% into +1.4%. CMG's consensus +19% EPS on +10.9% revenue requires margin repair none of its peers are being credited with.

Management credibility cuts both ways.
- **The sandbag pattern supports upside surprise.** FY2026 comp was guided 'about flat' with explicit 'conservative baseline' language (Q4 2025 and Q1 2026), then beaten twice and raised to low-single-digit.
- **Aspiration has run ahead of delivery.** The CEO's Q3 2025 pledge of a 'new strategy in 2026 that will give us back to mid-single-digit comp growth' has become low-single-digit.
- **The key 2027 question was dodged.** On Q2, when an analyst asked how investors know momentum continues into '27 given that CMG is 'doing a lot and spending a lot to get the 1% to 2%', the answer pivoted to a cyclospora statement.
- **HEEP lift has never been quantified.** 'I can't get into specifics at present' (Q3 2025).
- **Development proof points have been dropped.** The ~80% new-unit productivity and ~60% year-two cash-on-cash figures have not been repeated since Q3 2025.

None of this is fatal, but it is consistent with a team protecting the comp narrative while margin is the variable it is choosing to sacrifice. The 'Recipe for Growth' restructuring charges are immaterial ($2.1M in Q1, $3.3M in Q2), so there is no cost-takeout tailwind hiding there.

Ownership and news.
