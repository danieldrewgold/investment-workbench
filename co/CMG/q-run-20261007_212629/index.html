Key debate

Is the 2026 comp recovery (Q1 +0.5%, Q2 +2.2%) a durable transaction turn that sets up margin recovery and 19% EPS growth in 2027? Or is it LTO- and marketing-funded traffic on easy compares, now meeting renewed food-safety headlines, with margins structurally reset lower by reinvestment?

Where we differ from consensus

FY2027 revisions are 5 up and 0 down over 30 days. That drift came off the July guide raise, before the October 5 food-safety headlines, so the Street is extrapolating the raise. It is not reconciling three things: a near-zero 2026 traffic-plus-mix contribution, management's own statements that labor savings and price restraint are being reinvested, and a cash balance that, judging by shrinking interest income, can no longer fund $2.6B a year of buybacks. Management's worst track record is on exactly this kind of long-dated demand promise: FY2025 comps were first guided at 2 to 5 and came in at -1.7.

Narrative synthesis

Start with the numbers and a reconciliation the structured data forces on us. The FY2025 block shows diluted EPS of $1.09 on net income of $1,535.8M, with 1,265.4M diluted shares. Those three figures do not tie. $1,535.8M divided by 1,265.4M is $1.21, and the four reported GAAP quarters (Q1 $0.28, Q2 $0.32, Q3 $0.29, Q4 $0.25) sum to $1.14. The 1,265.4M figure is a recent point-in-time count consistent with Q2 2026: adjusted net income of $418.9M divided by $0.33 is about 1,269M shares. It is not the FY2025 weighted average, which was closer to 1,350M or more. Management's adjusted FY2025 EPS was $1.17, and consensus FY2026 of $1.15 shows YoY -1.7%, which only works against $1.17. So the Street is on an adjusted basis, and so are we. The structured gross margin of 25.4% is essentially restaurant-level margin; our quarterly average of 26.2%, 27.4%, 24.5% and 23.4% is about 25.4%. There is no GAAP versus non-GAAP gross margin gap to reconcile. The operating margin of 16.9% is after G&A, D&A, pre-opening and impairment.

The core diligence question is whether the comp turn is real. Reported comps run -0.4% (Q1 2025), -4.0% (Q2 2025, inferred from +3.0% revenue growth less about 7 points of new-unit contribution), +0.3%, -2.5%, then +0.5% and +2.2% in 2026. On a two-year basis, Q1 2026 stacked to about +0.1 and Q2 2026 to about -1.8. The Q2 headline was flattered by the softest compare of the cycle. Decomposed, Q2's 2.2% was 1.6 points of price, 1.0 of transactions and about -0.4 of mix. Q1 was about 0.9 price, +0.6 transactions and -1.0 mix. Traffic plus mix has contributed roughly zero this year; the comp is mostly price. For Q3, CFO Rymer guided 'somewhere around a plus 1%' with price in the 'mid-2% range'. He explicitly embedded the roughly 200bp late-July cyclospora softness for the whole quarter, so the guide implies traffic plus mix of about -1.5. Management has beaten near-term guides 11 of 11 times: Q1 by about 2 points (guided -1 to -2, printed +0.5) and Q2 by about 1.2 points (guided about +1, printed 2.2). Applying a 0.5 to 1.5 point beat gives a Q3 comp of 1.5% to 2.5%. Consensus Q3 revenue of $3,280M equals 3,003 x (1 + about 0.072 unit contribution + about 0.02 comp), so the Street already embeds the beat pattern. Our $3,264M is inside the $3,248M to $3,311M range. There is no Q3 revenue edge, and we say so.

The gap is FY2027. Consensus revenue of $14,443M is +10.9% on consensus FY2026. New units have added about 7.0 to 7.2 points of growth per quarter (Q1: 7.4% revenue less 0.5% comp; Q2: 9.3% less 2.2%), so the Street implies a FY2027 comp of about 3.7%. If price runs about 3%, matching inflation as management now promises 'from Q4 forward', that still requires traffic plus mix to swing from about zero in 2026 to roughly +0.7 or more. That would come against a year in which 2026's growth was bought with four standalone LTOs, a Rewards relaunch and marketing at 3% to 3.5% of sales (up 50 to 90bp). On the pitch-discipline questions: we cannot size how much of the 2026 comp came from new product versus core. Management's claim that protein LTOs add 'a few hundred basis points of transaction' is unverified. Honey Chicken is a repeat LTO and double-protein incidence sustained through April, so some is repeat behavior. Chipotle owns the fast-casual Mexican category, though a JPMorgan analyst flagged chicken and bowl competition. 2027 must lap that LTO cadence with equal news. Macro data cut both ways. Real disposable income is +6.0% YoY, but Michigan sentiment is 51.7 (-11.2% YoY), the saving rate has fallen to 4.1%, and food-away-from-home CPI (+3.4%) is running above food-at-home (+2.2%), which favors grocery. The guidance track record is decisive here.
