Thesis & brief

research/deep_research.py ↗

Key debate

The market is debating whether OXXO Mexico's Q1 2026 SSS recovery (+6% reported, with traffic still slightly negative) is durable and whether the Americas/Mobility segment's Brazil consolidation drag will compress consolidated margins enough to disappoint the FY2026 EPS consensus of $5.96 — a +108% YoY jump that embeds a very large implicit improvement in below-the-line items (interest expense normalization, tax, or one-time items) that the operating P&L alone cannot explain.

Where we differ from consensus

The FY2025 structured financials show net interest expense of $10,754M against operating income of $4,002M — meaning interest expense alone exceeds operating income by 2.7x, producing pre-tax income of roughly negative $6.75B before tax, which is inconsistent with the reported $1,077.8M net income and $4.56 EPS unless there are large below-the-line equity income contributions (KOF equity pickup), FX gains, or asset sale proceeds embedded in the FY2025 base. The consensus FY2026 EPS of $5.96 implies a ~$2B net income — a near-doubling — that requires either a dramatic reduction in net interest expense, a step-up in KOF equity income, or a one-time item; the FY2027 consensus then reverting to $5.30 (-11%) suggests the street itself is modeling a non-recurring FY2026 boost, but has not clearly disclosed what it is. This structural ambiguity in the below-the-line is the blind spot.

Narrative synthesis

FEMSA's reported financials present an immediate analytical puzzle that must be resolved before any forward estimate is credible. The FY2025 structured financials show net interest expense of $10,754M against operating income of $4,002M — a ratio of 2.7x, implying a pre-tax loss of approximately $6.75B. Yet reported net income is $1,077.8M and diluted EPS is $4.56. The reconciliation requires substantial below-the-line income — most likely equity income from the ~47% stake in Coca-Cola FEMSA (KOF), which as a separately listed entity with its own P&L contributes equity-method income to FEMSA's consolidated statement. In Q4 2025, KOF delivered revenue growth of 2.9% and operating income growth of 13.3% per the FEMSA earnings call; annualizing KOF's operating income trajectory and applying FEMSA's ~47% economic interest suggests equity income in the range of $5-8B pesos annually, which partially bridges the gap. Additionally, FEMSA's 'net interest expense' line likely includes FX translation losses and lease-related finance charges that are non-cash or partially offset by FX gains in other periods. The critical analytical point: the $10,754M 'net interest expense' figure is almost certainly not pure cash interest — it is a blended line that includes FX effects, lease finance charges, and potentially mark-to-market items. Sell-side analysts covering FMX as an ADR must navigate this complexity, and the wide EPS range ($5.24-$6.60 for FY2026, a 26% spread across just 4 analysts) confirms that the street itself has not converged on a clean model.

The FY2026 consensus EPS of $5.96 (+108% YoY from $4.56) is the central anomaly. A +108% EPS growth rate for a mature multi-format retailer with mid-single-digit revenue growth is extraordinary and demands explanation. Working backward from the consensus: if FY2026 revenue grows ~6.5% to approximately $49,700M (consistent with the quarterly run-rate — Q1 2026 actual was $11,780M, +6.1% YoY, implying a full-year of ~$49-50B), and if operating margins hold near the FY2025 level of 8.6%, operating income would be approximately $4,270M. To get from $4,270M operating income to ~$2,030M net income (340.7M shares × $5.96 EPS) requires the below-the-line drag to shrink from roughly $5,700M (FY2025 implied: $4,002M OI minus $1,078M NI, adjusted for taxes) to approximately $2,900M — a reduction of ~$2,800M. This is a very large implied improvement that the street has not explicitly articulated in any disclosed guidance. The most plausible mechanism is either: (a) a reduction in FX translation losses embedded in the 'net interest expense' line as the MXN stabilizes, (b) a step-up in KOF equity income as KOF's own profitability improves, or (c) a one-time item in FY2025 that depressed the base. Without management guidance to anchor this, the consensus is essentially a black box.

The FY2027 consensus EPS of $5.30 (-11% YoY from the FY2026 consensus of $5.96) is the tell. A company growing revenue at 7-8% annually does not see EPS decline 11% in the following year unless: (a) the FY2026 number embeds a non-recurring item that reverses, or (b) there is a structural cost headwind in FY2027 (Brazil consolidation losses scaling, Colombia overhead, Valora investment cycle). The sell-side's own model implies they are embedding something non-recurring in FY2026 — but with only 4 analysts covering FY2026 and 6 covering FY2027, and with EPS revisions running ↑4/↓0 for FY2026 but ↑1/↓2 for FY2027, the divergence in revision momentum itself signals that analysts are more confident in a near-term boost than in the sustainability of the FY2026 level. This is the structural edge: the FY2026 consensus is likely inflated by a non-recurring item, and the FY2027 consensus is the more honest baseline — but the market is currently pricing FY2026 as the new normal.

On the operating side, the OXXO Mexico recovery is real but fragile. Q1 2026 SSS of +6% (per the earnings call) was driven partly by excise tax pass-through on tobacco and soft drinks — management explicitly noted 'revenue growth in the tobacco and soft drink categories, reflecting the pass-through effect from the application of new excise taxes.' This is a price/mix tailwind, not a traffic recovery. Average traffic remained 'slightly negative' in Q1 2026, improving from the -3.1% in Q3 2025 and -0.6% in Q4 2025, but still not positive. CEO José Antonio Fernández Garza set a clear bar on the Q4 2025 call: 'success should mean same-store-sale growth in the OXXO Mexico level... added with market share growth' and 'a year with same-store sales traffic decline is a year that's a miss.' By that standard, Q1 2026 is still a miss on traffic, even as SSS looks healthy. The sequential trajectory discipline is important here: Q1 2026 revenue of $11,780M vs Q4 2025 of $12,478M is a -5.6% sequential decline. Prior year Q1 2025 vs Q4 2024 is not available in the corpus (only 5 quarters of data provided, with Q1 2025 as the earliest), so I cannot verify whether this seasonal pattern is normal or a break. I label this 'pattern unverified — no prior-Q4 2024 baseline available.' The YoY +6.1% is clean and consistent with the Q3 2025 inflection management described.

The Americas and Mobility segment is the most important margin swing factor for FY2026-2027. Q1 2026 operating margin for this segment was 1.1% — management noted this 'reflects the recent consolidation of OXXO Brazil, which generates an operating loss at this stage.' Brazil was consolidated in Q1 2026 (two months of results per the call), meaning FY2026 will carry a full year of Brazil losses. On the Q4 2025 call, management said Brazil needs 'around 1,000 stores' to cover overhead, and they are 'probably' at that level with confidence. Brazil same-store sales grew 6.9% in local currency in Q1 2026, which is encouraging, but the operating loss drag is real and will weigh on consolidated margins throughout FY2026. Colombia is 'very close to EBIT break-even' per management, and Latam ex-Brazil posted 13.1% currency-neutral SSS growth — these are genuine positives. But the net effect of full-year Brazil consolidation is a headwind to consolidated operating margins that the street may be underweighting, particularly given that the FY2026 EPS consensus already embeds a massive implied improvement in below-the-line items.

On the margin bridge: Q1 2026 gross margin was 40.5% vs 40.3% in Q1 2025 — a modest +20bps YoY improvement. Q4 2025 gross margin was 41.5%, the highest in the trailing 5 quarters, suggesting Q1 2026's sequential decline is partly seasonal. Operating margin in Q1 2026 was 6.9%, identical to Q1 2025 (6.9%), meaning zero operating leverage YoY despite +6.1% revenue growth. This is a yellow flag: if OXXO Mexico is recovering and management is executing overhead reduction ('we have just finished the same thing with FEMSA — on an FTE basis, I think we're done'), one would expect some operating leverage. The flat operating margin YoY in Q1 2026 suggests Brazil consolidation losses and Americas/Mobility investment are absorbing the efficiency gains from OXXO Mexico. The FEMSA Logistics-as-a-Service initiative (per the June 17 news item) is a nascent revenue stream that could improve asset utilization but is not yet material to the P&L.

On ownership and capital allocation: FEMSA's controlling shareholder is Mexican Economic Development Inc. with 74.78% of shares (per the 13D filing), making the float extremely thin — true float is likely under 25% of shares outstanding. This structural feature means: (a) price discovery is limited and consensus can persist even when wrong, (b) the 76.4% increase in short interest (to 1,256,450 shares, per the June 2 news) represents only 0.4% of total shares outstanding — this is not a meaningful short position and does not create squeeze dynamics. The director-level insider sale by Alfonso Garza Garza ($4.49M, 27.1% of his stake, March 2026) is worth noting — a director selling over a quarter of his position is a mild negative signal, though the absolute dollar amount is small relative to the company's market cap. UBS raised its price target to $139 from $122 in May 2026, maintaining Buy — this is the most recent analyst action and suggests at least one major bank is comfortable with the FY2026 consensus. The extraordinary dividend of 25.8 billion pesos approved at the AGM (on top of 15.2 billion pesos ordinary dividend) is a significant capital return signal — management is distributing capital rather than reinvesting it, consistent with the FEMSA Forward framework of returning ~$7.8B total between March 2024 and March 2027.

The bear case to this thesis is straightforward: the FY2026 EPS consensus of $5.96 may be correct if the 'net interest expense' line in FY2025 was inflated by a one-time FX loss or mark-to-market item that does not recur in FY2026. If MXN/USD stabilized or strengthened in 2026, the FX translation loss embedded in the interest line could reverse, producing a large below-the-line tailwind that legitimately drives EPS from $4.56 to ~$6. In that scenario, the FY2027 decline to $5.30 would reflect a normalization of FX gains rather than a non-recurring operating item. This is a plausible mechanism, and without explicit management guidance on the composition of the $10,754M net interest expense line, I cannot definitively rule it out. The evidence gap here is material: FEMSA does not provide formal forward guidance, and the structured financials do not decompose the interest expense line into cash interest, FX effects, and lease finance charges. Any analyst building a forward model without this decomposition is working with significant uncertainty — and the wide consensus EPS range ($5.24-$6.60) confirms this. My edge claim is therefore directional rather than point-estimate: the FY2026 consensus embeds assumptions that are not grounded in disclosed operating drivers, and the FY2027 consensus (-11% EPS) is internally inconsistent with a company growing revenue at 7-8% unless a non-recurring item is correctly identified and isolated.
