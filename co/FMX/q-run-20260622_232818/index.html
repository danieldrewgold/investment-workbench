Thesis & brief

research/deep_research.py ↗

Key debate

The market is debating whether OXXO Mexico's traffic recovery is durable (Q1 2026 showed 8.3% revenue growth and improving traffic trends after a difficult 2025) and whether the Americas/Mobility segment's consolidation of OXXO Brazil and LatAm expansion will dilute margins long enough to matter. The secondary debate is whether the $618.7M interest expense line — which dwarfs operating income of $230.3M — will normalize as FEMSA deploys its FEMSA Forward divestiture proceeds to delever.

Where we differ from consensus

The sell-side consensus for FY2026 EPS of $5.96 requires either a dramatic reduction in the $618.7M net interest expense line or a step-change in KOF equity income — neither of which is explicitly confirmed in the Q1 2026 transcript or structured financials. The FY2025 net income of $62M on $230M operating income implies the interest/tax wedge consumed ~$168M of pre-tax income, and the 38.7% tax rate further compressed the residual; for EPS to reach $5.96 on ~350M diluted shares, net income would need to reach ~$2.1B — a figure that implies either massive interest expense reduction or a structural change in how KOF equity income flows through. The FY2027 consensus EPS decline of -11.1% is equally puzzling given management's explicit capital return program (41B pesos in ordinary + extraordinary dividends approved at the AGM) and ongoing deleveraging, which should be EPS-accretive, not dilutive.

Narrative synthesis

FEMSA's financial architecture is unusual enough that it demands a first-principles reconciliation before any forward estimate can be trusted. The structured financials show FY2025 revenue of $2,683.8M, operating income of $230.3M (8.6% margin), and net income of only $62M — implying a pre-tax income of roughly $101M after the $618.7M net interest expense, then taxed at 38.7% to arrive at ~$62M. This math is internally consistent but raises an immediate question: how does consensus get to FY2026 EPS of $5.96? On approximately 350M diluted ADS shares outstanding (the standard FMX ADS count), $5.96 EPS implies net income of roughly $2.09B — a 33x increase from the $62M FY2025 base. This is not a rounding error; it is a structural discontinuity that demands explanation.

The most likely explanation is that the consensus EPS figures are being computed on a per-unit basis that reflects FEMSA's Mexican peso-denominated share structure (approximately 17.4B shares outstanding at the Series B/D level), not the ADS count. At 17.4B shares, $5.96 EPS would imply net income of ~$103.7B pesos — which at a ~17-18 peso/dollar exchange rate translates to roughly $5.8-6.1B USD net income. That is clearly not what the structured financials support either. The more parsimonious read is that the consensus EPS figures are in Mexican pesos per share (not USD per ADS), and the YoY growth rates (+108.5% for FY2026, -11.1% for FY2027) reflect the base effect of a very depressed FY2025 earnings year. This is a critical reconciliation point: the structured financials provided ($62M net income) are almost certainly the USD-consolidated figure, while the consensus EPS anchors ($5.96, $5.30) are peso-denominated per-share figures. Without explicit share count and currency denomination confirmation, any EPS-level edge claim carries material uncertainty — this is flagged as a primary evidence gap.

Setting aside the currency/denomination ambiguity, the operational story from the transcripts is clearer and more actionable. OXXO Mexico — the crown jewel, contributing the majority of Proximity Americas revenue — showed a genuine inflection in Q1 2026. In the Q1 2026 call, CEO José Antonio Fernández Garza reported 8.3% total revenue growth, same-store sales of 6%, and 158 net new store additions. Gross margin expanded 140 basis points to 46.2%, driven by supplier cooperation, distribution income, and warehouse savings. Critically, selling expenses grew 'in line with store expansion plus inflation despite double-digit growth in labor costs' — a direct quote from the Q1 2026 call — indicating that the efficiency program initiated in 2H 2025 is producing measurable results. Operating income grew 'well into the double digits.' This is a meaningful acceleration from Q4 2025, where same-store sales for Proximity Americas were 4.4% and traffic was -0.6%. The Q1 2026 data point of 6% SSS with improving traffic (management noted traffic was 'slightly negative' but 'improved significantly versus the declines we faced last year') represents a genuine sequential improvement.

Applying the sequential trajectory discipline: Q1 2026 SSS of 6% vs. Q4 2025 SSS of 4.4% is a +160bps sequential improvement. The prior-year comparison (Q1 2025 vs. Q4 2024) is not directly available in the corpus with precise numbers, but the Q3 2025 call noted that Q3 2025 was 'the first quarter to show positive same-store sales growth since the middle of last year,' implying Q1 2025 and Q2 2025 were negative. This means the Q1 2026 +6% SSS is lapping a negative Q1 2025 base — the easy-comp effect is real and material. The pitch discipline for SSS requires addressing: (1) what % came from new product vs. core — management cited tobacco and soft drink excise tax pass-through as a driver, plus financial services and coffee, suggesting a mix of regulatory pass-through (~2-3pp) and genuine category recovery; (2) repeat vs. trial occasion — coffee and financial services are high-frequency repeat occasions, which is favorable; (3) category ownership — OXXO owns the convenience channel in Mexico with ~20,000+ stores, so this is not new entry; (4) management investment — the Q4 2025 call described automated coffee machines, breakfast initiatives, and affordability programs as active investments; (5) comp drag from lapping — the Q1 2026 easy comp (lapping negative Q1 2025) will become a harder comp in Q1 2027, which is the structural risk to the FY2027 consensus.

The Americas & Mobility segment is the most complex and most misunderstood piece. Q1 2026 revenues grew 12.9% (10.5% comparable, currency-neutral), with OXXO LatAm ex-Brazil posting 13.1% currency-neutral SSS growth in Chile, Peru, and Colombia — a standout number. Brazil posted 6.9% SSS in local currency. The operating margin was only 1.1%, reflecting the consolidation of OXXO Brazil (which generates an operating loss at this stage). Management was explicit in the Q4 2025 call that Colombia should reach EBIT break-even in 2026 and that Brazil needs ~1,000 stores to cover overhead (currently below that threshold). This segment is a structural drag on consolidated operating margins in the near term but represents the most significant long-term growth optionality. The consensus revenue estimate for FY2026 of $895,283M (in pesos, given the scale) implies ~6.5% growth — directionally consistent with the Q1 2026 trajectory but potentially conservative given the Brazil consolidation tailwind and LatAm SSS momentum.

The interest expense line is the single most important variable for EPS and the one the street is most likely modeling incorrectly. FY2025 net interest expense of $618.7M against operating income of $230.3M means FEMSA is running a deeply negative interest coverage ratio on a reported basis. The FEMSA Forward program divested ~$11B in assets between 2023 and 2025, and the Q3 2025 call confirmed the capital allocation framework targets distributing ~$7.8B through March 2027 via ordinary and extraordinary dividends plus buybacks. The Q1 2026 AGM approved 15.2B pesos in ordinary dividends plus 25.8B pesos in extraordinary dividends — totaling ~41B pesos (~$2.3B USD at current rates). This capital return program is being funded from the divestiture proceeds, which implies the gross cash/investment position is declining. The critical question — unanswerable from the corpus — is whether the $618.7M interest expense reflects gross interest on legacy debt, net of interest income on the divestiture proceeds held in cash/investments. If a large portion of the $618.7M is interest income being netted against gross interest expense (i.e., the net figure is artificially high because FEMSA holds large cash balances earning below its debt cost), then as the extraordinary dividend deploys that cash, the net interest expense could actually INCREASE, not decrease. This is the bear case to the consensus EPS recovery thesis.

On the ownership and capital structure side: Mexican Economic Development Inc. holds 74.78% of FEMSA (per the 13D filing), making this a tightly controlled family company with minimal float-driven price discovery. The 76.4% increase in short interest reported in June 2026 (to 1,256,450 shares, or ~0.4% of outstanding) is notable in absolute terms but trivial as a percentage of float — this is not a squeeze setup, and the short interest increase likely reflects hedging activity around the ADS structure rather than a fundamental short thesis. Director Alfonso Garza Garza sold $4.49M of stock (27.1% of his stake) in March 2026 at an average price of $10.73 — this is a meaningful insider disposition and warrants attention, though the price ($10.73) appears to be in pesos per share rather than USD per ADS, which would be consistent with the currency denomination ambiguity noted above. UBS raised its price target to $139 from $122 in May 2026, maintaining Buy — the most recent analyst action is constructive and post-dates the Q1 2026 results.

The Logistics-as-a-Service initiative (June 2026 news) is a genuine optionality item the consensus is unlikely pricing. FEMSA is monetizing its Coca-Cola distribution network by offering third-party logistics to FMCG and healthcare companies across Mexico and LatAm. This is a capital-light, high-margin revenue stream that leverages existing fixed infrastructure — the incremental margin on LaaS revenue should be materially above the consolidated 8.6% operating margin. However, this is early-stage and the revenue contribution in FY2026 is likely immaterial to the consolidated P&L. It is a FY2027+ story.

The bear case to the bull thesis is straightforward and serious: OXXO Mexico's Q1 2026 SSS of 6% is substantially driven by excise tax pass-through on tobacco and soft drinks (a one-time pricing event, not a structural traffic recovery), and the traffic metric remains slightly negative. If the excise tax pass-through effect fades in Q2-Q3 2026 and traffic does not recover to positive territory, the SSS trajectory could revert toward the 1-2% range seen in Q3 2025. Combined with the Americas/Mobility segment's ongoing operating losses from Brazil consolidation and the unresolved interest expense question, FY2026 EPS could disappoint relative to the +108.5% consensus growth expectation. The specific falsifier: Q2 2026 OXXO Mexico SSS below 4% and/or traffic remaining negative would confirm the easy-comp thesis and challenge the recovery narrative.
