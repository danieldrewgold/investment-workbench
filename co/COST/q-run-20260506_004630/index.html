Thesis & brief

research/deep_research.py ↗

Key debate

Whether Costco can maintain its consistent 6-7% comparable sales growth trajectory while executing aggressive warehouse expansion plans amid execution challenges in international markets.

Where we differ from consensus

No clear edge. Management's warehouse opening revision from 35 to 28 locations for FY26 appears already reflected in street estimates, and the consistent 6-7% comp sales trajectory aligns with consensus revenue growth expectations.

Narrative synthesis

Costco's business model continues to demonstrate remarkable consistency, with management repeatedly emphasizing their ability to deliver 'very consistent' comparable sales growth in the 6-7% range across multiple quarters. In the Q2 2026 call, management noted that 'overall, our results have been very consistent in that 6% or 7%' despite various external noise factors, and this pattern held through Q1 2026 when they cited being 'very consistent in that sort of 6% to 7% comp sales range over the last several quarters.' This consistency provides a strong foundation for modeling forward revenue growth, though it also suggests limited upside surprise potential.

The most material development has been management's revision of warehouse opening plans from 35 locations to 28 net new openings for FY26, disclosed in the Q1 2026 call. CEO Ron Vachris explained that 'delays with a couple of our buildings in Spain resulted in us revising our plan net new openings for fiscal year 26 down to 28.' However, management maintained their long-term expansion confidence, stating 'we continue to plan for 30 plus net openings per year in future years.' This revision represents a 20% reduction in near-term unit growth but appears to be execution-related rather than demand-driven, as management cited specific geographic delays rather than broader market concerns.

The warehouse expansion economics continue to improve meaningfully. Management disclosed that 'fiscal year 25 openings generating an annualized $192 million per warehouse of sales in the year of opening, that is up from $150 million for new warehouses opened just two years earlier.' This 28% improvement in new store productivity suggests the expansion strategy is becoming more efficient, with faster maturation times and higher initial sales volumes. Combined with management's comment that 'new warehouse expansion has allowed us to consistently drive top line revenue well in excess of our comparable sales and gain significant market share,' the unit economics support continued aggressive expansion despite the FY26 timing delays.

Digital momentum represents a significant growth driver that may not be fully captured in traditional retail metrics. Management introduced a new 'digitally enabled comparable sales' metric in Q4 2025, explaining that it 'will incorporate all sales that originated online, including our same-day delivery service fulfilled by Instacart, Uber Eats, and DoorDash.' This metric showed 22.6% growth in Q2 2026 and 20.5% growth in Q1 2026, substantially outpacing overall comparable sales. The digital channel reached 'more than $27 billion' in FY25, representing roughly 10% of total sales with accelerating growth rates. Management also highlighted strong digital engagement metrics, with 'site traffic in the quarter was up 32% and app traffic was up 45%' in Q2 2026.

Membership fee income continues to provide stable, high-margin revenue growth. The company reported membership fee income of $1.355 billion in Q2 2026, up 13.6% year-over-year, and $1.329 billion in Q1 2026, up 14% year-over-year. This recurring revenue stream provides earnings stability and supports the low-margin merchandise strategy. Management noted accelerated executive member growth, with 'executive members per week grew at an accelerated pace from 4Q to 1Q,' likely driven by extended shopping hours and enhanced benefits that create additional member value.

Operational metrics suggest healthy underlying business momentum despite some weather-related volatility. Traffic growth remained consistent at 3.1% worldwide in both Q1 and Q2 2026, while average transaction size increased 4.2% worldwide in Q2 (3.5% excluding gas and FX impacts). However, management noted some weather disruption in Q2, explaining that 'because of the weather we had in the northeast in particular, we had 55 warehouses that were closed for a full day and then took a couple of days for the local communities to get back up to sort of speed.' This suggests some near-term headwinds that may normalize in subsequent quarters.

The company's merchandising strategy continues to evolve toward higher-value items and unique offerings. Management highlighted selling '$150,000 Emerald Cup 5.8 carat diamond ring, a $20,000 Babe Ruth autographed baseball, and nearly 200 luxury Whisper golf carts at an average price of approximately $9,000' in Q2 2026. This suggests successful expansion into ultra-premium categories that could drive higher average transaction values and differentiate from traditional warehouse club positioning. Combined with strong performance in 'gold and jewelry, tires, majors, health and beauty, and small electrics,' the merchandising mix appears to be successfully driving both traffic and ticket growth.

Looking at the macro context, Costco's model appears well-positioned for the current environment. With the personal saving rate at 3.60% (down 29.4% year-over-year) and real disposable personal income up 5.6% year-over-year, consumers have both the capacity and inclination to spend. Food at home CPI inflation of 1.9% year-over-year remains manageable compared to food away from home at 3.8%, supporting Costco's bulk grocery value proposition. The company's focus on 'quality and value, and newness and exciting new items' aligns well with consumer behavior in an environment where 'members are willing to and have the capacity to spend' when value is delivered.
