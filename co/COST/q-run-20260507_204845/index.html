Thesis & brief

research/deep_research.py ↗

Key debate

Whether Costco can maintain its consistent 6-7% comparable sales growth trajectory amid execution challenges (warehouse opening delays) and whether the street's 8.7% FY26 revenue growth assumption properly accounts for the revised expansion plan.

Where we differ from consensus

The street appears to be extrapolating the FY26 warehouse opening shortfall (28 vs 35 planned) into FY27, but management explicitly reaffirmed '30 plus net openings per year in future years' and new warehouses are generating $192M annualized sales vs $150M two years ago. The execution delay in Spain appears isolated rather than systemic.

Narrative synthesis

Costco's business model continues to demonstrate remarkable consistency, with management reporting 'very consistent' comparable sales results 'in that 6% or 7%' range across multiple quarters. This consistency provides a strong foundation for forward estimates, though recent execution challenges in warehouse expansion require careful analysis of the growth trajectory.

The most material development affecting forward estimates is the revision of FY26 warehouse openings from 35 to 28 locations, disclosed in the Q1 2026 call. CEO Ron Vachris attributed this to 'delays with a couple of our buildings in Spain,' providing specific geographic attribution that suggests an isolated execution issue rather than systematic expansion challenges. Importantly, management simultaneously reaffirmed long-term expansion plans, stating 'we continue to plan for 30 plus net openings per year in future years.' This dual messaging—near-term revision with long-term confidence—creates a setup where the street may be over-extrapolating the FY26 shortfall into FY27 assumptions.

The unit economics of new warehouse expansion continue to improve materially. Management disclosed that 'fiscal year 25 openings generating an annualized $192 million per warehouse of sales in the year of opening, that is up from $150 million for new warehouses opened just two years earlier.' This 28% improvement in new store productivity over two years suggests the expansion strategy is becoming more efficient, not less. Combined with management's comment about 'improvements in the performance of our new buildings and a reduction in their time to maturity,' the data supports confidence in the expansion program's returns.

Digital momentum provides another growth vector that may be underappreciated by consensus. The company introduced a new 'digitally enabled comparable sales' metric in Q4 2025, which 'incorporates all sales that originated online, including our same-day delivery service fulfilled by Instacart, Uber Eats, and DoorDash.' This metric showed 22.6% growth in Q2 2026, with management noting that 'site traffic in the quarter was up 32% and app traffic was up 45%.' The digital channel now represents over $27 billion annually, suggesting meaningful contribution to overall growth that may not be fully captured in traditional comparable sales modeling.

Membership fee income continues to provide a stable, high-margin revenue stream with consistent double-digit growth. Q2 2026 membership fee income of $1.355 billion represented 13.6% year-over-year growth, while Q1 2026 showed 11.4% growth based on available metrics. Management noted 'executive members per week grew at an accelerated pace from 4Q to 1Q,' suggesting the premium membership tier is gaining traction. This recurring revenue base provides earnings stability and supports the company's ability to maintain low merchandise margins while investing in price competitiveness.

The traffic and transaction trends underlying comparable sales growth remain healthy. Q2 2026 showed traffic growth of 3.1% worldwide with average transaction up 4.2% (3.5% excluding gas and FX impacts). Management's commentary suggests member behavior remains resilient: 'when you deliver on those things, we're seeing members are willing to and have the capacity to spend.' The consistency of these metrics across quarters—traffic consistently in the 3% range, transactions up mid-single digits—supports the sustainability of the 6-7% comparable sales trajectory.

However, some operational headwinds merit attention. Management disclosed specific weather-related disruptions in Q2 2026: 'we had 55 warehouses that were closed for a full day and then took a couple of days for the local communities to get back up to sort of speed' due to Northeast weather. While management characterized this as not having 'a major impact,' the specificity of the disclosure (55 warehouses, full-day closures) suggests material operational disruption that could affect quarterly patterns.

The guidance framework provides additional confidence in the forward trajectory. Management has maintained consistent language about comparable sales performance, using identical phrasing across quarters: 'very consistent in that sort of 6% to 7% comp sales range' in Q1 2026 and 'very consistent in that 6% or 7%' in Q2 2026. This linguistic consistency, combined with the actual performance delivery, suggests management has good visibility into the business trajectory and is not seeing material deceleration in underlying trends.

Capital allocation priorities remain focused on growth investment rather than financial engineering. Management noted that 'capital expenditure grew significantly faster than sales for the first time actually for a while' in FY25, driven by 'the number of warehouses that we're looking to open in 2026, the remodel work that we're doing, preparing the depots for the e-commerce expansion.' This growth-oriented capital deployment, combined with the improving unit economics of new stores, supports the thesis that management sees attractive reinvestment opportunities.

The synthesis of these factors suggests consensus FY26 revenue estimates of $299B appear reasonable given the revised warehouse opening plan, but FY27 estimates of $322B (7.8% growth) may prove conservative if management successfully returns to the 30+ opening cadence with improved store productivity. The key variable is whether the Spain-related delays represent an isolated execution issue or signal broader expansion challenges—management's confident reaffirmation of long-term opening targets suggests the former.
