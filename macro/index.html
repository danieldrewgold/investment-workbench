Macro digest: where we are and where it's heading

Late-cycle expansion with an energy-driven headline inflation shock: CPI at 3.71% runs well above core at 2.76%, labor is steady but barely hiring, and long rates are climbing hard (10Y 5.27%, up 1.09 pts in 12 months) while the Fed sits at 3.75%.
Consumer
  • Spending power is thin. Real disposable income grows just 1.3% and hourly earnings at 3.02% trail 3.71% CPI, so real wages are negative.
  • The gap is funded by drawing down savings. Real spending grows 2.57%, about double real income, as the saving rate fell 1.1 pts in 12 months to 4.1%.
  • Goods diverge. Durables accelerate to 4.61% (+0.8 in 3 months) and restaurants re-accelerate to 5.37% (+1.13), while autos slump to 1.86% (-2.79) and gas stations at 20.94% soak up wallet share.
  • Credit stress is not the problem. Card delinquency eased to 2.85% and revolving credit grows a modest 3.37%, but sentiment is depressed at 51.7.
Inflation
  • Headline CPI at 3.71% is up 0.77 pts in 12 months, while core CPI at 2.76% has cooled 0.35 pts. This is an energy shock, not broad overheating.
  • PPI all commodities, which includes energy and metals, runs at 9.85%, but it is decelerating, down 2.34 pts in 3 months, along with headline CPI (-0.56).
  • The Fed cut 0.47 pts over 12 months to 3.75% but nudged up 0.12 in 3 months. With core under 3%, it has room to hold rather than chase headline.
Labor
  • Layoffs are absent. Initial claims sit at 197k, down 22k in 12 months, and unemployment holds at 4.2%.
  • Hiring has stalled. Payrolls grow only 0.31% YoY, and wage growth slowed to 3.02% (-0.83 in 12 months).
Growth
  • Real GDP runs at 2.2% annualized, down 1.8 pts in 12 months. That is trend-like but losing momentum.
  • Retail sales at 5.36% are inflated by gas station prices (20.94%). Industrial production is soft at 1.42%.
Rates & credit
  • The 10Y has jumped 0.72 pts in 3 months to 5.27%, and the curve steepened to 0.51%. This is a bear steepener driven by term premium and inflation risk.
  • High-yield spreads widened 0.36 pts in 3 months to 3.03%. They remain tight, but the trend has turned against risk.
Industry costs
  • Proteins are easing fast. PPI beef slowed to 3.09% (-12.87 in 3 months), processed poultry is deflating at -12.53%, and processed foods are flat at 0.14%.
  • Labor is the cost outrunning pricing. Leisure and hospitality wages at 3.91% exceed menu inflation (CPI food away from home) at 3.66%.
  • Occupancy is creeping up. Nonresidential lessor PPI is 3.5%, up 2.33 pts in 12 months and now roughly matching menu pricing.
  • Menu prices at 3.66% sit 1.01 pts above grocery inflation at 2.65%. That widens the value gap versus eating at home, though restaurant sales at 5.37% show traffic is holding for now.
Where it's heading
  • Headline CPI should drift toward core as energy cools, with gas station sales already down 5.95 pts in 3 months and PPI all commodities down 2.34.
  • Consumer spending slows toward real income growth (1.3%) once the 4.1% saving rate stops falling. Autos (-2.79 in 3 months) are the early tell.
  • Long yields at 5.27% will pressure housing, durables and HY refinancing. Expect spreads above 3.03% if payroll growth (0.31%) weakens further.
Investment implications
  • Overweight restaurants with protein-heavy menus. Beef and poultry input deflation (3.09% and -12.53%) against 3.66% menu pricing widens gross margins. Favor operators with labor leverage, since wages run 3.91%.
  • Underweight autos and rate-sensitive durables. Auto sales slowed to 1.86% and the 10Y at 5.27% raises financing costs.
  • Start layering duration in tranches at a 5.27% 10Y. Core CPI at 2.76% and stalled hiring cap how far real yields need to rise.
  • Stay up in quality in credit. HY at 3.03% does not pay for widening risk. Watch claims above 197k and the saving rate below 4.1% as triggers to cut risk.
