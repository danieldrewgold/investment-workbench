Research brief · AXTI — full output

AXT's business model is deceptively simple on the surface — make compound semiconductor substrates, sell them — but the economics are almost entirely determined by a single exogenous variable: how many Chinese export permits the Ministry of Commerce approves for InP shipments in a given quarter. To understand the edge, you have to start by quantifying how extreme this sensitivity is. In Q1 2025, with effectively zero InP permits outside China, GAAP gross margin was -6.4% on $19.4M revenue. By Q1 2026, with permits flowing and InP at $13.6M of $26.9M total revenue, GAAP gross margin recovered to 29.6%. The structured financials show FY2024 GAAP gross margin of 11.3% ($11.2M on $99.4M revenue) — but the investor deck discloses non-GAAP gross margin of 24.3% for FY2024. The gap is approximately 13 percentage points, or roughly $13M in adjustments on $99.4M revenue, which is material. Management does not explicitly reconcile this in the transcripts provided, but the non-GAAP figure strips stock compensation and likely some depreciation from cost of revenue — investors modeling off the non-GAAP figure are working with a structurally more flattering baseline. For the forward model, I use GAAP gross margin as the anchor and note that the Q1 2026 GAAP gross margin of 29.6% is the cleanest recent read on normalized economics with permits flowing.

The sequential trajectory discipline is critical here and the street appears to be getting it right directionally but potentially wrong on magnitude. Q4 2025 is not in our quarterly historicals window, so a table-verified Q1 2026 sequential comparison is unavailable from the structured data — the +16.9% sequential figure cited ($26.9M vs Q4 2025's $23.0M) relies on a Q4 2025 revenue figure sourced outside the historicals table and cannot be independently confirmed here. Prior year: Q1 2025 actual ~$19M vs Q4 2024 actual ~$25M = approximately -22.9% sequential (consistent with the -22.9% Q/Q shown in the table for Q1 2025). So Q1 2026 showed a dramatically better sequential pattern than Q1 2025 — this is not seasonal normalization, it is a genuine inflection driven by permit flow normalization. Management confirmed this explicitly: 'Export permits in our first quarter came in slightly better than our guidance and are off to a solid start in Q2' (Q1 2026 call, CEO). The guidance beat history reinforces this: Q3 2025 printed $28M vs a $19-21M guide (a $7-9M beat driven by permit approvals that were explicitly excluded from guidance), and Q1 2026 printed $26.9M vs a $26M point estimate (a modest beat). The pattern is that management guides conservatively to what permits are in hand, and beats when additional permits arrive — this is a structurally asymmetric upside setup for near-term quarters.

For Q2 2026, the street consensus is $34M revenue and $0.07 EPS. Management guided 'sequential revenue growth' with Q2 being 'our largest quarter for indium phosphide in AXT's history' from a $100M+ backlog. The Q2 consensus of $34M implies roughly $7M of sequential dollar growth from Q1's $26.9M — a 26% sequential step-up. To sanity-check this: Q1 2026 InP was $13.6M. If Q2 is the largest InP quarter in company history, we need a reference point. The historical quarterly financials show peak revenue of ~$40M in Q1 2022 and ~$39M in Q2 2022, when InP was a smaller fraction of the mix. Management's 'largest InP quarter in history' claim, combined with the $100M backlog, suggests InP alone could be $18-25M in Q2 2026, with GaAs (~$5-7M) and JV revenue (~$7-8M) adding another $12-15M. This triangulates to $30-40M total Q2 revenue, consistent with the $34M consensus midpoint. The $34M consensus appears reasonable and well-anchored to the guidance signal — this is not where the edge lives.

The edge lives in two places: FY2026 operating leverage and FY2027 revenue sustainability. On FY2026 operating leverage: the street has $0.30 EPS on $143M revenue. Working the math — $143M revenue at a blended gross margin of ~32-35% (assuming InP mix continues to improve through the year) = $46-50M gross profit. Non-GAAP OpEx has been running $7.5-8.6M/quarter = ~$32-34M annually. That implies non-GAAP operating income of $12-18M, or roughly $0.18-0.28/share on 65.4M diluted shares. Adding back the tax rate of 0% (confirmed in FY2024 actuals, consistent with NOL utilization) and netting interest expense of ~$1.3M, GAAP EPS would be modestly below non-GAAP. The street's $0.30 consensus sits at the high end of this range — achievable but not conservative. My view is that the FY2026 EPS consensus of $0.30 is approximately right, with upside risk if InP mix is higher than modeled (each $5M of incremental InP revenue at ~50% incremental gross margin adds ~$2.5M gross profit = ~$0.04/share). The EPS consensus is not obviously wrong, but the composition matters: the street appears to be modeling a relatively smooth quarterly ramp, while the actual delivery will be lumpy and permit-dependent.

The more interesting edge is on FY2027. The consensus calls for $219M revenue (+53% YoY from $143M) and $0.75 EPS (+147% YoY). This is an extraordinary growth assumption for a company whose revenue has been: $83M (FY2019), $95M (FY2020), $137M (FY2021), $141M (FY2022), $76M (FY2023), $99M (FY2024). The FY2022 peak of $141M was achieved with a very different product mix and without the current export permit regime. To get to $219M in FY2027, AXT would need to roughly double its current quarterly run-rate from ~$27-34M to ~$55M/quarter. This requires: (1) sustained permit flow at 2x current levels, (2) manufacturing capacity to support 2x current InP output, and (3) no inventory digestion at customers who have been building buffer stock. Management has not disclosed a specific capacity expansion plan with dollar amounts or timelines — the deck mentions 'AXT is strategically adding capacity' (Deck p.5) but provides no quantification. The Q1 2026 call dodged the long-term supply agreement question entirely ('Tim? I'll follow up' — the entire recorded answer). This is a material evidence gap: the $219M FY2027 consensus requires a capacity ramp that management has not committed to publicly.

The insider selling pattern is the most significant material event in the corpus and deserves serious engagement rather than dismissal. Over the past 180 days: CEO Morris Young sold $37.5M of stock (20.7% of his pre-sale stake, 559,964 shares at avg $66.96); CFO Gary Fischer sold $21.3M (72.1% of his pre-sale stake — the most alarming figure); Director Jesse Chen sold $10.3M (76.1% of his pre-sale stake); Director David Chang sold $3.4M (51.2% of his pre-sale stake). Total insider selling: ~$72M across four insiders, with the CFO and two directors liquidating the majority of their holdings. The GuruFocus data confirms '31 insider sells and no insider buys over the past year.' This is not routine RSU-vest selling — these are open-market dispositions at prices ranging from $43 to $111/share. The CFO selling 72% of his stake is particularly notable: CFOs have the most granular view of near-term earnings trajectory and cash flow. The standard rebuttal ('insiders sell for many reasons — diversification, taxes, estate planning') is valid but weakened when the CFO liquidates 72% of his position. The stock's 4,600%+ trailing-year return provides a plausible diversification rationale, but the magnitude and breadth of selling across four insiders simultaneously is a yellow flag that the street appears to be discounting. Management has made zero reference to insider activity on any call — a management-silence flag that retail is actively debating.

The vertical integration risk flagged in the social topic analysis is a legitimate structural concern that management has never addressed on any call. Retail is debating whether COHR, LITE, and AAOI — which own foundries and have InP epi capabilities — could disintermediate AXT as a raw substrate supplier. The mechanism: if tier-one laser manufacturers and transceiver module makers (the exact customers AXT named as new wins in Q4 2025) vertically integrate into InP substrate production, AXT's 40% market share claim becomes vulnerable. Management's counter-argument — that crystal growth is 'very difficult' and AXT has 'substantial process technology and IP' (Deck p.3) — is qualitatively compelling but unquantified. The Seeking Alpha article from June 23, 2026 ('AXT: Market Is Pricing A Monopoly That Does Not Exist') appears to engage this thesis directly, though the full text is not in the corpus. This is a medium-term structural risk (2-4 year horizon) that the FY2027 consensus does not appear to price.

The credit market context is constructive (HY net buying, risk-on regime per FINRA data), and the macro backdrop — real disposable personal income up 5.8% YoY, Fed funds rate down 16.2% YoY — is supportive of risk assets broadly. PPI up 13.1% YoY is a potential input cost headwind for AXT's manufacturing operations, though management has not flagged this as a margin concern. The institutional crowding score of 0/100 (NORMAL) with only 9/47 tracked hedge funds holding the name suggests the stock is not yet crowded among sophisticated institutional investors — the 4,600% run has been driven more by retail momentum and index-passive flows (Vanguard added $29.9M, +45,304% in Q4 2025) than by concentrated hedge fund conviction. Short interest at 12% of float is meaningful but not extreme — the bear case has institutional representation but is not a crowded short.

Synthesizing: the near-term setup (Q2 2026, FY2026) is well-supported by the $100M backlog, permit normalization, and operating leverage math — the street's $34M Q2 revenue and $0.30 FY2026 EPS are achievable and the consensus is not obviously wrong. The edge is in FY2027: the $219M revenue consensus requires a capacity ramp and permit sustainability that management has not committed to, and the $0.75 EPS consensus requires margin expansion that assumes both volume scale AND OpEx discipline simultaneously. The strongest bear case to my own view is that the $100M backlog converts faster than expected (permits continue to flow, customers take delivery), AXT executes a capacity expansion that management has been quietly building toward, and the FY2027 consensus proves conservative rather than aggressive — in which case the stock's current valuation (implied ~130x FY2026 EPS at the $96.50 price target mean) is the real risk, not the earnings estimate. The mispricing, if it exists, is a DURATION_GAP: the street is pricing a multi-year monopoly-like earnings stream at a multiple that requires near-perfect execution, while the permit process, capacity constraints, and insider selling pattern all suggest execution risk is higher than the multiple implies.
