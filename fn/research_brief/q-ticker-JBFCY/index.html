Research brief · JBFCY — full output

Jollibee Foods entered FY2026 with a credible growth narrative — broad-based system-wide sales momentum, accelerating international expansion led by Compose Coffee and Jollibee International, and a Philippines business that management described as the 'undisputed and dominant market leader' with significant provincial penetration runway. The Q1 2026 results, however, delivered a sharp earnings shock: operating income fell -14.9% year-over-year against a full-year guidance of +15% to +20%, driven by a 330 basis point spike in cost of inventory as a percentage of revenue (from 55.5% to 58.8%) while the company took only a 0.4% average price increase — what CFO Richard Shin described on the Q1 2026 call as 'no price increase or very limited price increase, certainly below the inflation rates.' The gross margin compression of approximately 310 basis points (from 19.4% to 16.3%) is confirmed by the quarterly historical financials, which show Q1 2026 gross margin at 16.5% versus Q2 2025's 19.1% and Q1 2025's 18.5%. This is not a seasonal pattern — the sequential decline from Q4 2025 (18.1% GM) to Q1 2026 (16.5% GM) of approximately 160 basis points is materially worse than the Q4 2024 to Q1 2025 sequential, which showed Q1 2025 at 18.5% versus Q4 2024 (not available in the corpus, so the prior-year seasonal baseline is unverified). The pattern is directionally concerning but the prior-year Q4 2024 baseline is not in the corpus, so I label this conclusion as 'pattern partially verified — Q1 2025 vs Q4 2025 sequential available but Q4 2024 baseline absent.'

The central analytical question is whether the H2 2026 recovery management is promising is mathematically achievable. Starting from FY2025 operating income of $353.1M (per the structured financials), the +15% to +20% guidance implies FY2026 operating income of approximately $406M to $424M. Q1 2026 operating income came in at $69M (per the quarterly historical table, 5.5% operating margin on $1,251M revenue). To hit the low end of guidance ($406M), the remaining three quarters (Q2-Q4 2026) need to deliver approximately $337M of operating income combined — versus Q2-Q4 2025's implied $272M (FY2025 $353M minus Q1 2025 $81M). That requires Q2-Q4 2026 to grow approximately 24% year-over-year versus the prior-year comparable period. This is not impossible given pricing actions implemented in April, the Php 2.8Bn cost containment plan, and bean price normalization benefiting the coffee segment — but it requires a near-perfect execution of every lever simultaneously. The bear case is that April 2026 Philippines CPI printed 7.2% (disclosed on Deck p.25), sharply above JFC's own revised forecast of 6.4% and the BSP's 6.3% forecast, driven by an oil price shock from the US-Iran war. Using JFC's own disclosed sensitivity of +0.37 percentage points of inflation per $10/bbl increase in Dubai crude, a sustained $30/bbl oil shock (plausible in a US-Iran conflict scenario) adds approximately 1.1 percentage points to the inflation trajectory — pushing realized FY2026 inflation potentially to 7.5%+ versus the 6.4% assumption embedded in management's guidance. This is a concrete, named failure mode, not general caution.

The GAAP versus management-framing reconciliation is important here. The structured financials show FY2025 gross profit of $0.0M at an 18.5% margin — this appears to be a data artifact (gross profit is listed as $0.0M but the margin is 18.5%), suggesting the gross profit line in the structured data is a display error rather than a real zero. The quarterly historical table confirms gross margins of 16.5% to 19.1% across the last five quarters, which is consistent with management's disclosed Q1 2026 gross margin of 16.3% (from the transcript: 'GP margin compression from 19.4% to 16.3%, that is roughly 310 basis points'). The 30 basis point difference between the transcript's 16.3% and the quarterly table's 16.5% is within rounding tolerance and does not represent a material GAAP/non-GAAP gap. The operating margin of 7.1% in the FY2025 structured financials is consistent with the quarterly trajectory (Q1 2025: 7.0%, Q2 2025: 7.8%, Q3 2025: 6.8%, Q4 2025: 6.1%), confirming no material reconciliation issue between reported and management-cited figures.

The Shabu All Day acquisition represents the most underappreciated near-term earnings catalyst in the corpus. Management disclosed on Deck p.4 that the acquisition of approximately 170 stores represents 'potential +2% Revenue, +8% EBIT to the group.' Applying the +8% EBIT accretion to FY2025 operating income of $353.1M implies approximately $28M of incremental annual operating income — or roughly $7M per quarter once fully consolidated. The Korea FTC approval was announced April 16, 2026 (per the SEC Form 17-C filing), meaning this acquisition post-dates most consensus model updates. The +8% EBIT figure is management's own disclosed estimate, not an analyst inference, and it is directionally supported by the deal economics: acquiring Korea's largest all-you-can-eat hot pot chain at what management described as 'a very good multiple' (Q1 2026 call, CFO Shin) suggests the acquisition was not done at a premium that would dilute near-term returns. The key uncertainty is the consolidation timeline — the FTC approval was received but the acquisition closing date is not disclosed, so the quarterly phasing of the EBIT contribution is uncertain. However, the direction is clear: this is a positive earnings contributor that consensus has not had time to model.

On the international growth story, the data is genuinely impressive and the market appears to be discounting it due to the Philippines margin noise. Jollibee International SWS grew +19.2% in Q1 2026, outperforming Yum Brands (+6.0%), McDonald's (+11.0%), and Restaurant Brands (+6.2%) on a directly comparable peer table (Deck p.17). Compose Coffee is running at 30+ monthly store openings with a sub-1% closure rate across 3,015 stores and 31% SWS growth year-over-year (Deck p.20). Tim Ho Wan delivered c.3.6x EBITDA expansion at 22.5% topline growth ex-China (Deck p.21), with EBITDA expanding from 28.4 to 101.4 in absolute terms. These are not marginal improvements — they represent a portfolio of international brands with genuine momentum. The coffee segment also benefits from a specific near-term tailwind: CFO Shin noted on the Q1 2026 call that 'bean prices also have stabilized, and we've seen a decrease in bean prices, which is to say we expect even stronger results in the second half at the cost of goods line.' This is a concrete, commodity-specific tailwind for the coffee segment that partially offsets the Philippines food cost headwind.

Management credibility deserves careful assessment. The CFO's shift to 'quarter-by-quarter action plans' language on the Q1 2026 call — 'I didn't put rest of the year here. We're talking now quarter by quarter type of action plans here' — is a meaningful signal of reduced forward visibility, even as the formal FY2026 guidance of +15% to +20% operating income growth remains on the deck. This is a multi-signal credibility concern: the guidance is formally intact but the CFO's verbal framing has already walked away from it. The deck explicitly states 'Management is reassessing guidance to be disclosed by Q2' (Deck p.29), which means the Q2 2026 results will be the first formal test of whether the guidance is revised down. The Q1 2026 SSSG of 3.5% also missed the guided 4%-6% range by 50 basis points, and the operating income miss of -18.2% versus +15% to +20% guidance is a 33-38 percentage point gap — the largest miss in the track record table. Against this, management did provide specific, quantified attribution (330bps cost-of-inventory spike, 0.4% average price increase versus inflation) and confirmed that pricing actions were implemented in April, which is a more credible remediation narrative than generic 'macro headwinds.'

The macro context adds nuance. US headline CPI is running at +4.2% YoY (FRED, May 2026) and PPI at +13.1% YoY — the latter being the most relevant input cost signal for a food operator. Food away from home CPI is +3.5% YoY (BLS, May 2026), which sets the pricing ceiling for US QSR operators. Leisure and hospitality average hourly earnings are growing at +4.0% YoY (BLS, May 2026), outpacing food-away-from-home CPI by 50 basis points — a structural labor cost headwind for company-operated stores. For JBFCY specifically, the Philippines inflation dynamic is more acute: April 2026 actual inflation of 7.2% versus the 6.4% JFC internal forecast represents a 80 basis point upside surprise in a single month, driven by an oil price shock that has a quantified pass-through sensitivity (+0.37ppt per $10/bbl). The credit regime is risk-on (FINRA bond flow data shows HY net buying at $237M today and $333M 30-day average), which is a supportive backdrop for multiple expansion if the earnings recovery materializes — but it does not change the fundamental earnings math.

The bear case to the recovery thesis is straightforward and should be stated clearly: if April 2026 Philippines inflation at 7.2% is not a one-month spike but the beginning of a sustained elevated inflation regime driven by the US-Iran oil shock, then the 'moderate single digit price increase' management implemented in April may be insufficient to restore margins, and the Php 2.8Bn cost containment plan may be offset by ongoing input cost escalation. The specific falsifier is the Q2 2026 operating margin — if it does not show meaningful sequential improvement from Q1 2026's 5.5% toward the 7%+ range that would be consistent with the full-year guidance, the guidance cut at Q2 results will be larger than the market currently expects. The strongest bull counter-argument is that the pricing actions are already implemented (confirmed '[IMPLEMENTED]' on Deck p.30), the cost containment plan is in place, bean prices are declining (a concrete tailwind for the coffee segment), and the Shabu All Day EBIT accretion is additive regardless of Philippines margin dynamics. The setup is genuinely two-sided, but the asymmetry favors a guidance cut at Q2 rather than a guidance confirmation.
