## Thesis  _(sets the stance)_
At $30.90 CMG trades near 24x our below-consensus FY2027 EPS of $1.31, a de-rated multiple that already prices our margin view, leaving expected value about 3.5% above price and no edge until Q3 shows whether labor and margin turn.

What would create an edge: Long below about $27.80, where probability-weighted value of about $32 clears the 15% hurdle. Long also at or near $30 if the Oct 28 Q3 print shows labor as a percent of sales flat or down YoY and a Q4 restaurant-margin guide flat or up YoY, excluding the $27M Q4 2025 gift card true-up. That would lift bull odds to about 40% and EV to about $34.5. A short needs a price above about $37.60 with no margin evidence, or a Q3 transaction comp below 0% plus a Q4 margin guide down 150bp or more YoY.

At 22.6x consensus FY2027 EPS and 29.7x trailing, the market is paying the lowest multiple in five years. It paid 40-60x for 17-45% growth in 2022-2025. Read against 2026's 31x for flat EPS, the market already doubts the margin recovery and is pricing something close to our $1.31, not the street's $1.37.

**Verdict:** 23.6x next-FY P/E on our FY2027 EPS of $1.31 (22.6x on consensus $1.37) looks **fair** and is more likely to **hold**. The multiple-for-growth history has compressed steadily: 60.5x for +31.6% EPS growth in 2024, 40.8x for +17% in 2025, 31.3x for -0.5% in 2026. If FY2027 growth is the 14% we model, half of it from share shrink, a mid-20s P/E fits that path. Expansion needs proof that margin recovers. Management's 40% flow-through claim is refuted (S31), and the 'temporary dislocation' claims are unverified (S41, S79, S141). Four factors limit compression: FY2027 revisions are 5 up and 0 down, comp guides were beaten two quarters running, short interest is only about 4%, and the company keeps buying stock heavily. Hold is the base case.

- Q4 2026 restaurant margin up YoY excluding the 70bp gift card benefit in Q4 2025: the dislocation-recovery story is verified, so move bull probability to 40%+.

- Quarterly buybacks fall below $400M with the stock under $35: the share-shrink support for EPS weakens, so lower base EPS by about $0.02.
- A definitive acquisition or merger agreement announced, or a disclosed bid at 15% or more above the prior close: the earnings-multiple framework no longer applies, so close the analysis.

## Strongest counter
The red team argues the compression is mostly cyclical food cost plus deliberate under-pricing. On that view, HEEP reaching about 2,000 stores lifts FY2027 labor productivity, and the 50bp recovery consensus models is likely. It also argues the analyst's FY2026 number quietly assumes H2 improvement. The stance survives because it is not a short, and we agree the downside case is weak at this multiple. On attribution, the Q2 reported bridge leaves about 110bp outside food and marketing, and management itself says HEEP hours are reinvested. The red team's 12bp wage gap compares wages to CPI, not to Chipotle's own 2.5% price. Even granting half its point, the bull case sits at 25%, and EV of about $32 does not clear the long hurdle.

### A3. Analyst working notes (original brief narrative)
The setup. At $30.90 (Oct 6 close), CMG trades at 26.9x FY2026 consensus EPS of $1.15 and 22.6x FY2027 consensus of $1.37. That is close to Texas Roadhouse at 24.7x and well above Darden at 17.8x in the peer table. The question that sets the price is whether 2026 is a margin trough. The facts: GAAP operating margin was 12.9% in Q1 2026 versus 16.7% a year earlier, and 15.7% in Q2 versus 18.2%. Adjusted EPS fell 17% in Q1 and was flat at $0.33 in Q2. One reconciliation is needed first. The structured FY2025 gross margin of 25.4% matches the restaurant-level margin management cites (quarters of 26.2%, 27.4%, 24.5% and 23.4% average about 25.4%), because Alpha Vantage's cost of revenue includes all restaurant operating costs. So there is no GAAP versus non-GAAP gross margin gap to bridge. EPS is a different story. Structured FY2025 diluted EPS is $1.09, while management's adjusted figure is $1.17, and consensus FY2026 at $1.15 (-1.7% YoY) is clearly on the adjusted basis. The structured share count of 1,265.4M also does not reconcile to net income of $1,535.8M divided by $1.09, which implies about 1,409M weighted shares. The 1,265M looks like a current-period count, and it matches Q2 2026 GAAP net income of about $405M divided by $0.32. We model from about 1,265M in Q2 2026, falling to about 1,190M on average in FY2027 on the current buyback pace.

Revenue and sequential trajectory. Q2 2026 revenue of $3,349M rose 8.4% sequentially. The prior-year Q2 sequential was +6.5% (Q2 2025 $3,063M vs Q1 2025 $2,875M) and two years ago it was +10.0%. So Q2 2026 was 1.9pp better than 2025's seasonal pattern, which is a real improvement, consistent with comps moving from 0.5% to 2.2%. Stripping out comps, the non-comp unit contribution has been steady: 7.2% in Q3 2025, about 6.5% in Q4 2025 after removing the $27M gift card true-up, 6.9% in Q1 2026 and 7.1% in Q2 2026. Q3 consensus of $3,280M (+9.2% YoY) therefore implies a comp of about 2%. That is a -2.1% sequential step from Q2, in line with 2025's -2.0% (Q3 2025 vs Q2 2025) and far milder than 2024's -6.0%. Management guided Q3 comps to 'around a plus 1%' and said that guide already assumes the cyclospora drag continues, after a roughly 200bp softening in late July. On two-year stacks, H1 2025 comps averaged about -2.3% (inferred from FY2025 -1.7%, Q3 +0.3% and Q4 -2.5%), so H1 2026's average of about 1.35% is a two-year stack near -1%. A 1% Q3 comp on a +0.3% compare is already a 2.3pp two-year acceleration. Consensus at 2% assumes even more. The beat history argues for consensus: management beat its Q1 guide of -1% to -2% with +0.5%, and its Q2 guide of about +1% with 2.2%. Against that, food-safety headlines returned on Oct 5 and the August salmonella lawsuit is still live. We model Q3 revenue at about $3,245M (comp about 0.8%, -3.1% sequential). Even so, Q3 EPS comes out near $0.29, because buybacks absorb the revenue gap. Q3 is in line, not an edge.

The FY2027 revenue ask. Consensus FY2027 revenue of $14,443M is +10.9%. With unit contribution running about 7%, that implies a comp of roughly 3.5-4%, against a 2026 we model at about 1.5% (Q1 0.5, Q2 2.2, Q3 about 0.8, Q4 about 3.0). The bull case leans on three things, all category (e) narrative. First, HEEP stores 'comp hundreds of bp better', with 200-400bp cited in Q1. Second, the rollout reaches about 2,000 stores by year-end and finishes in 2027. Third, a national catering launch in 2027. We tested the comp engine against the five SSS questions. (1) New product versus core: Q2 transactions rose only 1.0%. Management says protein LTOs alone 'typically generate a few hundred basis points of transaction width over the life of the promotion', and Honey Chicken attachment was 'north of 25%'. That suggests core traffic outside the LTOs may be flat to negative, though we cannot prove it. (2) Repeat versus trial: the engine is four LTOs a year, which is a treadmill that 2027 has to lap. (3) Category ownership: Chipotle owns fast-casual Mexican, but analysts flagged chicken and bowl competition, and CAVA's consensus revenue growth is +27.5%. (4) Investment: marketing rose 90bp in Q3 2025, 50bp in Q4, 40bp in Q1 and 30bp in Q2. These are category (c) actions, and Q1 marketing dollars were up about 22%. (5) Lap drag: Q2 2027 laps a 2.2% comp built on Honey Chicken, the Rewards relaunch and Summer of Extras. Mix is also still a drag. In Q1, mix cost about 100bp and check fell 0.1% despite roughly 0.7% price. In Q2, check rose 1.2% on about 1.6% price. Macro is mixed. Michigan sentiment is 51.7 (-11.2% YoY) and the saving rate is 4.1% (-21% YoY), but credit card delinquencies are down 6.2% and unemployment is 4.2%. Food-away-from-home CPI (+3.4%) is running ahead of food-at-home (+2.2%), and the CEO himself said in Q3 2025 that lost visits go 'to grocery and food at home'. We use a 2.5% FY2027 comp and about $14,190M of revenue.

The margin bridge is where we disagree most. Management's story is a 150bp price-versus-inflation 'dislocation' (Q4 2025 call) that is 'temporary' and gone by Q4 2026, when 'those 2 to match'. The reported numbers do not fit that story. In Q2, restaurant margin fell 220bp. Cost of sales explained only 80bp and marketing 30bp, so about 110bp came from labor, occupancy and other costs. The CFO attributed the labor increase to 'wage inflation and performance bonuses in addition to labor execution in restaurants as we lean into the guest experience'. The CEO said, as a statement against interest, that HEEP labor savings are being reinvested, not banked: 'we are reinvesting the two to three hours of efficiency back into our restaurants'. BLS leisure and hospitality wages are up 3.9% YoY. Chipotle's menu pricing is about 2.5% in Q3, and we expect roughly 2.5-3% in 2027. On a labor line near 25.5% of sales, that gap alone is worth about 20-35bp of deleverage a year unless transactions grow well above 1%. The CEO's Q3 2025 promise to expand margins at 'around 40%' flow-through 'regardless of what's going on with the economy' is refuted by three straight quarters of YoY operating-margin decline: Q4 2025 -50bp (14.1% vs 14.6%), Q1 2026 -380bp (12.9% vs 16.7%), and Q2 2026 -250bp (15.7% vs 18.2%), all while revenue grew roughly 5-9% YoY. The Q1 decline is the most alarming at nearly four full percentage points, and even the comparatively modest Q4 2025 deterioration of 50bp occurred against a backdrop of positive revenue growth. Below the restaurant line, costs are growing faster than sales. Restaurant-level profit minus operating income was $322M in Q1 2026 versus $275M (+17%) and $319M in Q2 versus $279M (+14%). Pre-opening costs rose to 0.5% of revenue from 0.3%, impairment and closure costs to 0.4% from 0.2%, and adjusted G&A grew 10% to $176.2M. The macro input data adds risk, not comfort: PPI all commodities is up 9.9% YoY, while management's guide has H2 food inflation stepping down to about 3%. On our numbers, FY2026 restaurant margin lands near 23.9%, about 150bp below FY2025. Consensus FY2027 EPS of $1.37 back-solves to restaurant margin of about 24.3-24.4%, meaning a 40-50bp recovery. We hold 23.8%.

The EPS bridge and capital return. Our FY2027 model: revenue $14,190M, restaurant margin 23.8% gives $3,377M; non-restaurant costs grow about 8% to about $1,395M; operating income is about $1,982M (14.0%); interest income is about $25M as cash funds buybacks; tax is 24%; average diluted shares are about 1,190M. That yields about $1.28 GAAP and about $1.30-1.31 after typical adjustments. Consensus is $1.37 and the range is $1.27-1.45. The buyback is doing heavy lifting on both sides. Management bought $687M at $42.39 in Q3 2025, $742M at $34.14 in Q4 2025, $701M in Q1 2026 and $631M at $32.55 in Q2 2026, and the board added $1.8B and then $1.3B of authorization. All of those purchases are underwater at $30.90. That is a confident, money-backed signal that management sees the shares as cheap. It is not a sign of a shrinking runway, because unit growth stays near 350 a year. It also means EPS growth runs about 5pp ahead of net income growth. Consensus net income growth for FY2027 is only about 11-12%, roughly in line with revenue, so the EPS debate is really about margin. Tax works in the stock's favor. Management guided 25-27% in each of FY2023-FY2025 and delivered 24.2%, 23.7% and 23.6%, missing low every time (n=3, bias -1.3pp). The FY2026 guide is 24-26%, and the Q2 adjusted rate was 24.0%. If any of the street sits at the 25% midpoint, a 24% rate adds about $0.02 to FY2027.

Credibility signals. The guidance track record has two sides. On FY comps, management sandbagged 2026 ('about flat', then 'low single-digit') but missed FY2025 by 5.2pp (guided 2-5%, delivered -1.7%), so the shrunk bias is only +0.08pp. On units it reliably hits the high end, at 345 against a 315-345 guide in 2025. Three signals matter for FY2027. First, the ~80% new-restaurant productivity and ~60% year-two cash-on-cash figures, last cited in Q3 2025, have disappeared. A garbled Q1 2026 Q&A fragment refers to a '40% return on investment in year two', and we cannot tell whether that describes Europe or North America. Second, the Q2 2026 lead question was effectively 'you are spending a lot to get 1-2% comps, why will 2027 be better'. The CEO answered with a cyclospora food-safety statement instead. Third, the CEO's Q3 2025 promise of 'mid-single-digit comp growth' in 2026 became a flat guide one quarter later and is now 'low single digit'. Tone has improved to 'confident', and QTD language was positive for three straight calls. But the one forward data point that matters most, 'trends have been softer in recent weeks', was the last thing management said about Q3.

News, ownership and positioning. The October tape is noisy. Shares fell 4-6.3% on Oct 5 on returning food-safety headlines, with unusually high put buying, then bounced on Oct 6 on Chipotle-Starbucks merger rumors that management has not addressed. We put little weight on the merger talk. A combination of that size has no corpus support, and a retail post says it well: the company is probably 'too big to be taken over'. Ownership needs a correction. The ownership block shows Pershing Square at 4.2%, but that comes from a 2020 13D/A. The 13F flow shows Pershing fully exited in Q4 2025 (-$844M) and Viking exited (-$260M). Third Point opened a new $175M position at 2.4% of its fund, a real conviction bet, while Citadel and Millennium added positions that look more like trading. Short interest is only about 4% of float, so this is not a crowded short and there is no squeeze setup. The only open-market insider sale, by the controller, was $0.1M at $35.75 on Aug 21, which is immaterial. Credit markets are risk-on, with HY buying on both the day and the 30-day average. So the recent slide looks partly mechanical (sector selling, headline risk) and partly fundamental (margins genuinely down 200bp+).

Bear case to our view, and the mispricing read. The strongest case against us is the evidence of execution momentum. Max-15 throughput has accelerated for two straight quarters, linebacker deployment passed 70% of restaurants, HEEP reaches about 2,000 stores by year-end, and every European country comped high single digits in Q2. If HEEP really adds 200-400bp in equipped stores and coverage rises from about 25% to about 75% of the base on average in 2027, that alone is 1-1.5pp of system comp. Add catering, and a 4% comp with 24.5% restaurant margin and $1.40+ EPS is reachable. Management has also beaten its last two quarterly comp guides by 1.2-2pp. Our view changes if three things happen at the late-October Q3 print (the Q3 2025 call was Oct 29): Q3 comps print at 2% or better despite cyclospora, labor as a percent of sales is flat YoY, and the Q4 restaurant-margin guide is flat or up YoY after excluding the gift card true-up. The second test is the FY2027 framework on the Q4 call in early February 2027. On the mispricing, we do not see a 'better and cheaper' setup. The stock is cheaper than its own history at about 23x our FY2027 number, but the fundamentals have not yet improved at the margin line. We expect FY2027 estimates to drift down about 4-5% as the Q4 margin guide fails to show the promised recovery. Most of that is already reflected in the multiple, so we are neutral on the stock and below consensus on FY2027 EPS.

**Business:** Chipotle Mexican Grill runs roughly 4,200 company-owned fast-casual Mexican restaurants, mostly in North America, plus a small and growing set of partner-operated international units in the Middle East and Mexico. Almost all revenue is food and beverage sold in its own restaurants. About 38% of sales come through digital channels. Growth comes from opening about 350 restaurants a year, around 80% of them with a Chipotlane, and from same-restaurant transactions and price.

### A4. Red team verdict on the analyst brief
Reject. The pitch's FY26 estimate silently assumes the H2 margin recovery its FY27 thesis denies, it pins transitory food-cost compression on labor while ignoring the HEEP rollout and Chipotle's deliberate under-pricing, and its 4% FY27 EPS edge rests on an unsourced consensus and is smaller than unmodeled buyback and tax swings, all expressed as an outright short into a 52-week low with live M&A speculation.
